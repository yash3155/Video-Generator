CHAPTER 8: SECURITIZATION AND THE CREDIT CRISIS OF 2007
Author: John C. Hull (Fundamentals of Futures and Options Markets)

Core Section Structure:
8.1 Securitization:
- The origin-to-distribute banking model vs traditional mortgage lending.
- Creation of Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) and Asset-Backed Securities (ABS).
- Tranche construction and the cash-flow waterfall rules:
  * Senior Tranche: 75% to 80% of principal, AAA-rated, receives interest and principal first. Subordinated to lower tranches to absorb initial losses.
  * Mezzanine Tranche: Typically 15% to 20% of principal, rated BBB or AA, receives cash flows after the Senior tranche. Absorbs losses once the Equity tranche is wiped out.
  * Equity Tranche: First-loss piece (typically 5% of principal), unrated, pays high yield if defaults remain low, but wiped out immediately by the first 5% of portfolio losses.
- The ABS CDO (Collateralized Debt Obligation):
  * Repackaging BBB mezzanine tranches from multiple ABS into a secondary structure.
  * Creating new Senior (AAA), Mezzanine (BBB), and Equity tranches from pools composed entirely of BBB-rated subprime risk.
  * The catastrophic alchemy: Turning portfolios of BBB mortgage bonds into 75% AAA-rated CDO debt!

8.2 The U.S. Housing Market:
- The subprime mortgage expansion (2000–2006).
- Low interest rate environment following the dot-com crash.
- Relaxed underwriting standards: No Income No Job or Assets (NINJA) loans, stated income loans, subprime loans with zero down-payment.
- Teaser rates: Low fixed rates for first 2–3 years resetting to floating rates at substantially higher spreads.
- Originators made upfront loan fees and immediately securitized the loans, creating severe agency problems and misalignment of incentives (no skin in the game).

8.3 What Went Wrong?
- House price bubble bursting: Between 2000 and 2006, U.S. house prices increased dramatically. In 2006, prices peaked and then plummeted across the entire country.
- Correlation breakdown: Risk managers assumed regional independence (if California defaults, Florida or Ohio might not). When national house prices dropped, default correlations surged toward 1.0.
- Mathematical Loss Wipeout:
  * In a standard ABS with 5% Equity, 15% Mezzanine, 80% Senior:
    - If collateral losses reach 10%: The 5% Equity tranche is completely wiped out (100% loss). The Mezzanine tranche suffers 5% loss out of its 15% (33.3% loss). The Senior tranche remains intact.
    - If collateral losses reach 20%: Equity (5%) is 100% wiped out; Mezzanine (15%) is 100% wiped out!
  * In an ABS CDO created from BBB tranches:
    - Because the underlying assets are ALL mezzanine tranches, once collateral losses exceed 5%, the entire asset pool suffers losses, resulting in near-total wipeouts of even AAA-rated ABS CDO tranches!
- Rating Agency Incentives: Rating agencies (S&P, Moody's, Fitch) were paid by the bond issuers (conflict of interest) and relied on historical default models that had never seen a nationwide housing crash.

8.4 The Aftermath & Regulatory Reforms:
- Business Snapshot 8.1: The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision and Capital Accords:
  * Basel I (1988) and Basel II (2004) capital frameworks had substantial gaps for trading book assets and securitization tranches.
  * Basel II.5 (2009) and Basel III (2010):
    - Substantially increased Tier 1 equity capital ratios.
    - Added the Capital Conservation Buffer (2.5%) and Countercyclical Buffer.
    - Introduced the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) and Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR).
    - Enforced mandatory skin-in-the-game rules (5% risk retention by mortgage originators/securitizers under the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act).
    - Mandatory central clearing (CCPs) for standardized OTC derivatives and trade repository reporting.
