### 1. Basic Tenets of the Elliott Wave Principle

* **Core Definition & Three Aspects**: The Elliott Wave Principle analyzes market movements through three primary factors: **pattern, ratio, and time**, in that strict order of importance [1-3].
* **The 8-Wave Cycle Structure**: Market movement follows a repetitive rhythm consisting of a **5-wave advance** followed by a **3-wave decline**, forming one complete cycle of **8 waves** [4-6].
  * **Impulse Waves**: Waves **1, 3, and 5** move in the direction of the main trend [4].
  * **Corrective Waves**: Waves **2 and 4** move against the main trend to correct waves 1 and 3 [4]. The main 5-wave advance is followed by 3 corrective waves labeled **a, b, and c** [4].
* **Subdivisions and Degrees of Trend**:
  * Elliott categorized **9 distinct degrees of trend** (ranging from a Grand Supercycle spanning two centuries down to a Subminuette degree covering a few hours) [7].
  * The direction of the next larger wave determines how a wave subdivides [8, 9]. Waves moving in the direction of the next larger trend subdivide into **5 waves**; counter-trend waves subdivide into **3 waves** [6, 8, 9].
  * **Fundamental Rule**: A corrective phase can **never take place in 5 waves** (corrections are 3s, except for triangles) [6, 9, 10].
* **Fibonacci Wave Counts**: Complete wave cycles subdivide according to the Fibonacci sequence: **2, 8, 34, and 144 waves** [5, 8, 11].
* **Rules of Impulse Waves**: Wave 4 must **not overlap Wave 1** [3, 12]. Wave 3 is **never the shortest** of the three impulse waves [13].

---

### 2. Connection Between Elliott Wave and Dow Theory

* **Historical Background**: R.N. Elliott was heavily influenced by Dow Theory and noted in a 1934 letter that the Wave Principle served as a "much needed complement to the Dow Theory" [14, 15].
* **Structural Parallels**:
  * Dow Theory’s **3 major advancing phases** in a bull market correspond directly to Elliott’s **3 impulse waves (Waves 1, 3, and 5)** separated by 2 corrective waves (Waves 2 and 4) [15, 16].
  * Both theories drew inspiration from oceanic wave action: Charles Dow compared market trends to tides, waves, and ripples [15, 17], while R.N. Elliott referenced market "ebbs and flows" [15].
* **Confirmation Rule Difference**: Dow Theory strictly requires **two averages** (Industrials and Transports) to confirm each other [16]. Elliott Wave Theory requires signals in **only a single average** [16].

---

### 3. Corrective Wave Types

#### A. Zigzags (5-3-5)
* **Definition & Structure**: A sharp 3-wave corrective pattern against the main trend that breaks down into a **5-3-5 sub-wave sequence** [18].
  * Wave A has 5 sub-waves, Wave B has 3 sub-waves, and Wave C has 5 sub-waves [18].
* **Pattern Characteristics**: Wave B falls short of the starting point of Wave A, while Wave C extends well beyond the end of Wave A [18].
* **Double Zigzag Variation**: Occurs in larger corrective structures and consists of **two 5-3-5 zigzags connected by an intervening a-b-c pattern** [18].

#### B. Flats (3-3-5)
* **Definition & Structure**: A sideways consolidation pattern following a **3-3-5 sub-wave sequence** [19]. Wave A contains 3 sub-waves (unlike the 5 sub-waves in a zigzag), Wave B contains 3, and Wave C contains 5 [19].
* **Variations**:
  1. **Normal Flat**: Wave B rallies to the top of Wave A, and Wave C terminates at or just below the bottom of Wave A [19].
  2. **Irregular Flat**: Wave B exceeds the starting peak of Wave A, and Wave C drops past the bottom of Wave A [20].
  3. **Inverted Irregular (Running Flat)**: Wave B reaches or exceeds the top of Wave A, but Wave C fails to reach the bottom of Wave A, signaling strong underlying market momentum [20].

#### C. Triangles (3-3-3-3-3)
* **Occurrence & Role**: Usually forms in **Wave 4** preceding the final impulse move of the trend (or as Wave B of an a-b-c correction) [6, 21].
* **Structure**: A 5-wave sideways consolidation (a-b-c-d-e) where each internal wave subdivides into 3 sub-waves [6, 22, 23].
* **Four Varieties**: Ascending, Descending, Symmetrical (Contracting), and Expanding (Reverse Symmetrical) [23].
* **Trading & Confirmation Rules**:
  * Triangles indicate a resumption of the trend, but also warn that after one final impulse wave (Wave 5), the overall market move will peak [21].
  * The 5th and final wave inside a triangle sometimes produces a **false trendline breakout** before the true thrust occurs in the original trend direction [24].
* **Target & Timing Objectives**:
  * **Price Target**: The thrust after breakout is expected to cover a distance equal to the **widest part (height/base) of the triangle** [25].
  * **Time Target**: The **apex** (where the two converging trendlines meet) often marks the timing point for the final Wave 5 termination [25].

---

### 4. Rule of Alternation

* **Core Concept**: The market rarely executes the exact same pattern twice in succession; it indicates what pattern is **unlikely to recur** [26, 27].
* **Application to Corrections**: If Wave 2 is a **simple** correction (e.g., a 5-3-5 zigzag), Wave 4 will likely be a **complex** pattern (e.g., a flat or triangle) [26, 28]. Conversely, if Wave 2 is complex, Wave 4 will tend to be simple [26, 28].

---

### 5. Channeling

* **Purpose**: Used to establish price target objectives and confirm correct wave counts [28].
* **Construction Steps**:
  1. **Initial Channel**: Draw a baseline connecting the bottoms of Wave 1 and Wave 2, then project a parallel line over the top of Wave 1 [28].
  2. **Secondary / Accelerated Channel**: If Wave 3 accelerates past the upper channel line, redraw lines along the top of Wave 1 and the bottom of Wave 2 [29].
  3. **Final Channel**: Draw a base support line under the troughs of **Wave 2 and Wave 4**, then project a parallel resistance line over the peak of **Wave 3** [29].
* **Trading Rule**: Wave 5 should approach or touch the upper line of the final channel before terminating [30].

---

### 6. Wave 4 as Support

* **Core Rule**: In a bear market following a 5-wave bull advance, price declines will usually **not fall below the trough of the previous Wave 4 of one lesser degree** [3, 30].
* **Trading Objective**: The low of the preceding Wave 4 functions as a reliable support floor and provides a maximum downside price target [30].

### 7. Mathematical Formulas, Variables, and Definitions

#### A. Core Fibonacci Ratios
* **Golden Ratio (\\(\phi\\))**: Ratios of consecutive sequence numbers approach **\\(0.618\\) (\\(61.8\%\\))** [31].
* **Inverse Golden Ratio (\\(1/\phi\\))**: Inverse ratio of consecutive sequence numbers approaches **\\(1.618\\) (\\(161.8\%\\))** [2].
* **Alternate Ratios**: Ratios of alternate sequence numbers approach **\\(0.382\\) (\\(38.2\%\\))** and **\\(2.618\\) (\\(261.8\%\\))** [2].

#### B. Impulse Wave Price Target Formulas
1. **Wave 3 Minimum Target**:
   \\[\text{Target}_{\text{W3 Top}} = \text{Price}_{\text{W2 Bottom}} + (1.618 \times \text{Length}_{\text{W1}})\\] [13]
   * *Variables*: \\(\text{Length}_{\text{W1}} = \text{Price}_{\text{W1 Top}} - \text{Price}_{\text{W1 Bottom}}\\).
2. **Wave 5 Target (from Wave 1)**:
   \\[\text{Target}_{\text{W5 Top}} = \text{Price}_{\text{W1 Top or Bottom}} + (3.236 \times \text{Length}_{\text{W1}})\\] [13]
   * *Variables*: \\(3.236 = 2 \times 1.618\\).
3. **Wave 5 Target (Extended Wave 5 with W1 \\(\approx\\) W3)**:
   \\[\text{Target}_{\text{W5 Top}} = \text{Price}_{\text{W4 Bottom}} + (1.618 \times \text{Distance}_{\text{W1 Bottom to W3 Top}})\\] [32]

#### C. Corrective Wave & Retracements Formulas
1. **Zigzag Wave C Target**:
   \\[\text{Length}_{\text{WC}} \approx \text{Length}_{\text{WA}}\\] [32]
   \\[\text{Target}_{\text{WC Bottom}} = \text{Price}_{\text{WA Bottom}} - (0.618 \times \text{Length}_{\text{WA}})\\] [32]
2. **Flat Wave C Target**:
   \\[\text{Length}_{\text{WC}} \approx 1.618 \times \text{Length}_{\text{WA}}\\] [33]
3. **Triangle Wave Proportions**:
   \\[\text{Length}_{\text{Wave}_n} \approx 0.618 \times \text{Length}_{\text{Wave}_{n-1}}\\] [33]
4. **Percentage Retracements**:
   * Minimum Retracements (Strong Trend): **\\(38.2\%\\)** [33].
   * Standard Retracements: **\\(50\%\\)** [33, 34].
   * Maximum Retracements (Weak Trend): **\\(61.8\%\\)** [33].

### 8. Author Market Applications & Observations

* **Stocks vs. Commodities**:
  * **Wave Extensions**: Wave 3 most commonly extends in stock market averages, whereas **Wave 5 most commonly extends in commodity futures** [12].
  * **Overlap Rules**: The rule that Wave 4 cannot overlap Wave 1 is rigid in stock markets, but brief intraday penetrations can occur in commodity futures charts [12].
* **Market Suitability**: Because the Wave Principle relies on mass investor psychology, it works best on broad stock market indices (such as the Dow Jones Industrial Average) and heavily traded commodities like **gold** [3, 12, 35]. It is less reliable on individual stocks or thinly traded futures [3, 12, 35].
* **Author Case Studies (Time Targets)**:
  * **Treasury Bonds (1981 Low)**: Monthly Fibonacci time targets counted forward from the 1981 bottom in T-Bonds coincided with major trend reversals [34, 36].
  * **Dow Jones Industrial Average (1982 Low)**: Monthly time targets measured from the 1982 Dow bottom successfully landed on key bear market/reversal years in **1987, 1990, and 1994** (with the 1987 top occurring exactly 13 years after a major low) [34, 36].

Fibonacci Percentage Retracements The preceding ratios help to determine price objectives in both impulse and corrective waves. Another way to determine price objectives is by the use of percentage retracements. The most com-monly used numbers in retracement analysis are 61.8% (usually rounded off to 62%), 38%, and 50%. Remember from Chapter 4 that markets usually retrace previous moves by certain predictable percentages—the best known ones being 33%, 50%, and 67%. The Fibonacci sequence refines those numbers a bit further. In a strong trend, a minimum retracement is usually around 38%. In a weaker trend, the maximum percentage retracement is usually 62%. FIBONACCI TIME TARGETS We haven't said too much about the aspect of time in wave analysis. Fibonacci time relationships exist. It's just that they're harder to predict and are considered by some Elliotticians to be the least important of the three aspects of the theory. Fibonacci time targets are found by counting forward from significant tops and bottoms. On a daily chart, the analyst counts forward the number of trading days from an important turning point with the expectation that future tops or bottoms will occur on Fibonacci days—that is, on the 13th, 21st, 34th, 55th, or 89th trading day in the future. The same technique can be used on weekly, monthly, or even yearly charts. On the weekly chart, the analyst picks a significant top or bottom and looks for weekly time targets that fall on Fibonacci numbers. (See Figures 13.27 and 13.28.)"
