### Basic Cycle Concepts

Time cycle analysis introduces the dimension of **time** to technical charts, shifting the focus from solely *how far* a market will move to *when* a market turn or move will occur [1, 2]. Cycles are measured from **trough to trough** (low point to low point) because troughs are more distinct, stable, and reliable than crests [3, 4].

#### Core Definitions & Dimensions

*   **Amplitude**: The height of a wave measured from trough to crest [5, 6]. It is expressed in price units such as **dollars, cents, or points** [5].
*   **Period**: The time elapsed between two consecutive troughs [5, 6]. It is expressed in time units such as **days, weeks, months, or years** [5, 7].
*   **Phase**: The time location of a wave trough [5]. **Phase difference** measures the time distance between the troughs of two distinct waves [8, 9].

#### Mathematical Representation & Formulas

*   **Summation Formula**: The price movement \\(C(t)\\) at any time \\(t\\) is the mathematical sum of all active component cycles acting on that market [10]:
    \\[\text{Composite Price } C(t) = A(t) + B(t) + \dots + N(t)\\]
    where \\(A(t), B(t), \dots\\) represent the individual cyclic waves active at time \\(t\\) [10, 11].
*   **Ideal Halfway Peak Location**:
    \\[\text{Ideal Crest Time} = t_{\text{trough}} + \frac{\text{Period}}{2}\\]
    *(Note: Ideal peaks occur at the 50% midpoint of the period, though actual market peaks shift due to translation)* [4].

### Principles of Cyclic Analysis

J.M. Hurst established fundamental principles that describe how cyclic waves interact to create chart patterns, trend channels, and market structure [10, 12, 13]:

| Principle | Core Concept & Definition | Trading / Charting Implications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Summation** | All price action is the simple addition of all individual active cycles [10]. | Complex chart patterns (such as Head and Shoulders or Double Tops) are created by combining shorter and longer duration cycle waves [10, 14, 15]. |
| **Harmonicity** | Neighboring cycles are related by small, whole numbers—most commonly a factor of **2** [11]. | A 20-day cycle has a 10-day shorter harmonic and a 40-day longer harmonic [11]. |
| **Synchronicity** | Waves of differing lengths tend to reach their troughs (bottoms) at approximately the same time [16]. | When cycles synchronize at a low, price bottoms are sharper and more forceful [15, 16]. Also applies to related markets bottoming together [16]. |
| **Proportionality** | A cycle's **amplitude** is directly proportional to its **period** (length) [17]. | Longer-term cycles display wider price swings (a 40-day cycle has about twice the amplitude of a 20-day cycle) [17]. |
| **Variation** | Cyclic behavior displays strong tendencies rather than rigid mathematical rules [17]. | Allows for minor real-world timing distortions and variations from ideal mathematical models [17]. |
| **Nominality** | A baseline set of harmonically related cycle lengths influences virtually all markets [18]. | Standard cycle model spans: **18 years \\(\rightarrow\\) 54 months (3-yr exception) \\(\rightarrow\\) 18 months \\(\rightarrow\\) 9 months \\(\rightarrow\\) 6 weeks \\(\rightarrow\\) 4 weeks \\(\rightarrow\\) 2 weeks \\(\rightarrow\\) 1 week** [7, 18]. |

### Dominant Cycles

**Dominant cycles** are cycle lengths that consistently impact price movements and can be isolated for market forecasting [19].

#### Cycle Classification Hierarchy

1.  **Long-Term Cycles**: Cycles lasting **2 or more years** [20].
2.  **Seasonal Cycle**: An annual cycle lasting **1 year (12 months)** [20].
3.  **Primary / Intermediate Cycle**: Ranges from **9 to 26 weeks** (roughly 3 to 6 months) [20, 21].
4.  **Trading Cycle**: A **4-week cycle** (28 calendar days / 20 trading days) [20-22].
5.  **Alpha and Beta Cycles**: Subdivisions of the 4-week trading cycle, averaging **2 weeks** (10 trading days) each [20, 21].

#### Notable Cycles

*   **The Kondratieff Wave**: A long-term **54-year economic cycle** discovered by Nikolai D. Kondratieff that exerts major long-term influence across commodity prices, interest rates, stocks, and wholesale prices [20].
*   **The 28-Day Commodity Trading Cycle**: A dominant **4-week (20 trading days)** cycle prevalent across commodity and stock markets, often linked to lunar influences [22, 23]. This 20-day cycle forms the mathematical rationale behind popular technical tools like the **5, 10, 20, and 40-day moving averages** and Donchian's **4-Week Rule** [23, 24].

### Left and Right Translation

**Translation** describes the shifting of cycle crests (peaks) to the left or right of the ideal midpoint of a cycle's period [4].

```
Uptrend (Bullish):      Trough /---- Crest ----\ Trough   (Right Translation)
Downtrend (Bearish):    Trough / Crest ----\ Trough       (Left Translation)
```

#### Definitions & Confirmation Rules

*   **Ideal Midpoint**: In an ideal 20-day cycle (trough to trough), the peak occurs on **day 10** [4].
*   **Right Translation**:
    *   **Definition**: The cycle crest shifts to the **right of the midpoint** (e.g., peaking after day 10) [25].
    *   **Market Meaning**: Prices spend more time rising than falling [25].
    *   **Implication**: Indicates a **bullish** market environment driven by an uptrend in the next longer-degree cycle [25, 26].
*   **Left Translation**:
    *   **Definition**: The cycle crest shifts to the **left of the midpoint** (e.g., peaking before day 10) [25].
    *   **Market Meaning**: Prices spend more time falling than rising [25].
    *   **Implication**: Indicates a **bearish** market environment driven by a downtrend in the next longer-degree cycle [25, 26].

#### Trading & Timing Rules

1.  **Trend Alignment**: Always identify the direction of the **next longer-degree cycle** first [21, 27].
2.  **Long Positions**: When the longer-degree cycle is trending up (**right translation** expected), use troughs in short-term trading cycles as buy points [21, 25].
3.  **Short Positions**: When the longer-degree cycle is trending down (**left translation** expected), use crests in short-term trading cycles as sell/short points [21, 25].

### Seasonal and Stock Market Cycles

#### Seasonal Cycles

All markets exhibit annual seasonal tendencies driven by weather, agricultural harvest, or supply/demand cycles [28, 29]:

*   **Soybeans**: Usually peak between **April and June** and bottom around harvest between **August and October** [29].
*   **Copper**: Tends to bottom in **October/February** and peak during **April/May** [30, 31].
*   **Crude Oil**: Tends to peak in **October** and turn upward in **March** [30, 31].
*   **U.S. Dollar**: Tends to form a low point in **January** [31].
*   **Treasury Bonds**: Typically hit major highs in **January**; prices are generally weaker during the first half of the year and stronger during the second half [31].

#### Monthly Stock Market Tendencies

*   **Strongest 3-Month Window**: **November through January** [32].
*   **Strongest Month**: **December** (concluding with the "Santa Claus rally") [32].
*   **Weakest Month**: **September** [32].

#### The January Barometer

*   **Core Rule**: *"As January goes, so goes the year"* [33].
*   **Definition**: The direction of the **S&P 500 Index** during the month of January predicts the overall direction of the stock market for that entire calendar year [33].
*   **5-Day Variant**: The performance of the S&P 500 during the **first 5 trading days of January** offers an early directional indicator for the year [33].
*   **Distinction**: Must not be confused with the *January Effect*, which refers specifically to small-cap stock outperformance in January [33].

#### The Presidential Cycle (4-Year Cycle)

Stock market performance tracks the 4-year U.S. presidential election cycle, yielding distinct historical average returns (tracked by Hirsch's *Stock Trader's Almanac* since 1904) [34, 35]:

| Year in Presidential Cycle | Historical Market Character | Average Gain (since 1904) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Year 1: Election Year** | **Strong** [35] | **+224%** cumulative [35] |
| **Year 2: Post-Election Year** | **Weak** [35] | **+72%** cumulative [35] |
| **Year 3: Midterm Year** | **Weak** [35] | **+63%** cumulative [35] |
| **Year 4: Pre-Election Year** | **Strong** [35] | **+217%** cumulative [35] |

- "THE JANUARY BAROMETER According to Hirsch: "as January goes, so goes the year." The well known January Barometer holds that what the S&P 500 does during January will determine what kind of year the market as a whole will have. Another variation on that theme is the belief that the direction of the S&P 500 during the first 5 trading days of the year gives some hint of what's ahead for the year. The January Barometer shouldn't be confused with the January Effect, which is the tendency for smaller stocks to outperform larger stocks during January."

- "THE PRESIDENTIAL CYCLE Another well known cycle that affects stock market behavior is the 4 year cycle, also called the Presidential Cycle, because it coincides with the elected term of U.S. presidents. Each of the 4 years has a different historical return. The election year (1) is normally strong. The postelection and midyears (2 and 3) are normally weak. The preelection year (4) is normally strong. According to Hirsch's Trader's Almanac, election years since 1904 have seen averages gains of 224%; postelection years, gains of 72%; midterm years, gains of 63%; and preelection years, gains of 217%.
