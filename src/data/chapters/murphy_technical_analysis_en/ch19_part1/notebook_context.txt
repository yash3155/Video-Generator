### 1. Murphy's 20-Point Technical Checklist

Murphy presents his checklist in Chapter 19 ("Pulling It All Together—A Checklist") as a systematic framework to ensure technical analysts ask the right questions and evaluate all key tools before taking a trading position [2-4].

#### Core Concept & Rationale
Market analysis requires synthesizing multiple technical indicators [2]. No single indicator works in all market conditions; success relies on knowing which tools to emphasize in the current situation and seeking clues that others might overlook [2, 5].

#### The Technical Checklist (Base-Checking Questions)
1. **Overall Market Direction:** What is the direction of the overall market? [5]
2. **Sector Trends:** What is the direction of the various market sectors? [5]
3. **Long-Term Perspective:** What are the weekly and monthly charts showing? [5]
4. **Trend Classification:** Are the major, intermediate, and minor trends moving up, down, or sideways? [6]
5. **Support & Resistance:** Where are the key support and resistance levels? [6]
6. **Lines & Channels:** Where are the important trendlines or channel lines? [6]
7. **Volume & Open Interest:** Are volume and open interest confirming price action? [6]
8. **Percentage Retracements:** Where are the 33%, 50%, and 66% retracement levels? [6]
9. **Gaps:** Are there any price gaps, and what type are they (breakaway, runaway, exhaustion)? [6-9]
10. **Reversal Patterns:** Are there any major reversal patterns visible (e.g., Head and Shoulders, Double/Triple Tops or Bottoms)? [6, 10]
11. **Continuation Patterns:** Are there any continuation patterns visible (e.g., Triangles, Flags, Pennants, Rectangles)? [6, 11-13]
12. **Price Objectives:** What are the price targets projected from these patterns? [6, 14]
13. **Moving Averages:** Which way are the key moving averages pointing? [6]
14. **Oscillator Status:** Are oscillators (RSI, Stochastics, MACD) in overbought or oversold territory? [15-18]
15. **Divergences:** Are any momentum or oscillator divergences apparent? [15, 19]
16. **Contrary Opinion:** Are contrary opinion metrics (e.g., Sentiment Readings, Commitments of Traders) showing extremes? [15, 20, 21]
17. **Elliott Wave Structure:** What is the Elliott Wave pattern indicating? [15, 22]
18. **Wave Counts:** Are there obvious 3-wave or 5-wave patterns? [15, 22]
19. **Fibonacci Metrics:** Where are the primary Fibonacci retracements or time projections? [15]
20. **Cycle Timing:** Are any cycle tops or bottoms due? [15]
21. **Cycle Translation:** Is the market demonstrating right or left translation? [15]
22. **System Direction:** Which way is the computerized trend-following system pointing? [15]
23. **Micro-Structure Signals:** What are Point & Figure charts or Japanese Candlesticks signaling? [4]

#### Post-Analysis Trade Execution Rules
Once a bullish or bearish conclusion is established, Murphy outlines 8 operational questions before executing [4]:
* **Market Outlook:** Which way will the market trend over the coming months? [4]
* **Action:** Am I buying or selling? [4]
* **Position Size:** How many units will I trade? [4]
* **Risk Limit:** How much capital am I prepared to risk if wrong? [4]
* **Profit Target:** What is my specific profit objective? [4]
* **Entry Level:** Where will I enter the market? [4]
* **Order Type:** What type of order will I use (market, limit, stop)? [4]
* **Stop Placement:** Where will I place my protective stop-loss? [4]

### 2. Coordinating Technical and Fundamental Analysis

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Fundamental Analysis:** Examines economic forces of supply and demand to determine a security's *intrinsic value* [23]. If intrinsic value is above current price, the asset is bought; if below, it is sold [23].
* **Technical Analysis:** Studies market price action (price, volume, open interest) to forecast price trends [24, 25].
* **Primary Premise:** Price action discounts all known and unknown fundamentals [26, 27]. Price acts as a **leading indicator** of conventional fundamental wisdom [27].

#### Technical vs. Fundamental Dynamic
| Dimension | Fundamental Analysis | Technical Analysis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focus** | Cause of price movement [28] | Effect of price movement [28] |
| **Primary Utility** | Asset selection / broad trend direction [23, 29] | Precise entry/exit timing and risk control [29-31] |
| **Market Lead Time** | Lags behind price action [27] | Leads conventional fundamental data [27] |

#### Coordination Rules & Practical Application
1. **Timing Requirements:** Even if fundamental analysis determines *what* to buy or sell, timing the entry and exit points relies on technical analysis [29, 31].
2. **Fundamentalist Risk Filter:** Fundamental traders can use trend-following technical systems or chart analysis as a filter to prevent entering positions against prevailing market trends [32, 33].
3. **Technician News Alert:** Technicians should monitor fundamental context to anticipate major market drivers [32]. Observing price reaction to fundamental events provides strong technical signals:
   * **Rule:** If a market fails to rally on bullish fundamental news when in an overbought condition, it indicates a potential technical top and selling opportunity [32, 34].
4. **Early Warning Signals:** Sharp, unexplained chart movements alert fundamental analysts that unreleased fundamental data is being discounted by the market [32, 33].

### 3. Chartered Market Technician (CMT)

#### Definition & Role
The **Chartered Market Technician (CMT)** is a professional designation administered by the **Market Technicians Association (MTA)** [35, 36]. It establishes formal credentials, professional standards, and a code of ethics for practitioners of technical analysis [35, 36].

#### Key Details
* **Examination Structure:** A rigorous **three-step examination process** that tests proficiency across technical charting, quantitative indicators, system design, and market theory [35].
* **Professional Parity:** Designed to provide technical analysts with the credentialing equivalent of the CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst) in fundamental security analysis or the CPA/CTA in accounting and trading [35].
* **Testing Curriculum:** Murphy's work (*Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*) serves as a primary source text in the CMT testing process [37].

### 4. Final Course Summary

#### Core Philosophical Tenets
1. **Market Action Discounts Everything:** All fundamental, political, and psychological factors affecting supply and demand are directly reflected in market prices [26].
2. **Prices Move in Trends:** Price action moves in directional trends that persist until definitive reversal signals occur [26, 38, 39].
3. **History Repeats Itself:** Chart patterns reflect human psychology, which remains consistent over time [40, 41].

#### Summary Trading & Money Management Rules
Murphy synthesizes the critical guidelines for practical trading and capital preservation [42-44]:

* **Trend Alignment:** Always trade in the direction of the intermediate trend [42]. Buy dips during uptrends; sell bounces during downtrends [42].
* **Loss Management:** Cut losses short and let profits run [42]. Always set protective stop-loss orders upon entry [42].
* **Reward-to-Risk Ratio:** Ensure a minimum projected **3 to 1 reward-to-risk ratio** on candidate trades [42].
* **Pyramiding Guidelines:** When scaling into winning positions:
  1. Make each successive position layer smaller than the previous one [43].
  2. Add only to winning positions—never average down into a losing position [43].
  3. Trail protective stops to the breakeven point as positions gain [43].
* **Capital Risk Allocation:** Limit total account exposure to prevent catastrophic drawdown (e.g., risk no more than 5% of account equity per trade commitment) [45]. Never meet a margin call [43].
* **Top-Down Timeframe Analysis:** Begin analysis on long-term charts (monthly and weekly) to identify macro trends, then consult daily charts for setup identification, and use intraday charts for precise entry timing [44, 46, 47].
* **Intermarket Linkages:** Account for intermarket relationships: the U.S. Dollar impacts Commodities, which drive Bonds, which in turn influence Equities and sector rotation [48, 49].
* **Trading Discipline:** Keep rules simple, follow a structured trading plan, ignore media commentary, and remain comfortable operating against public consensus [4, 44].

Once the analyst knows where the market is from a longer range perspective, he or she gradually "zeros in" on the shorter term. The first chart to be considered is the 20 year monthly chart. The analyst looks for the more obvious chart patterns, major trend-lines, or the proximity of major support or resistance levels. He or she then consults the most recent five years on the weekly chart, repeating the same process. Having done that, the analyst narrows his or her focus to the last six to nine months of market action on the daily bar chart, thus going from the "macro" to the "micro" approach. If the trader wants to proceed further, intraday charts can then be consulted for an even more microscopic study of recent action."
- "FROM DAILY CHARTS TO INTRADAY PRICE CHARTS Because timing deals with very short term market action, intraday price charts are especially useful. Intraday charts are indispensable for day trading purposes, although that's not our focus here. We're mainly interested in how intraday activity can be used to aid the trader in the timing of purchases and sales once the basic decision to enter or exit a market has been made. Analysis begins with monthly and weekly charts for long term perspective. Then the daily chart is consulted, which is the basis for the actual trading decision.
