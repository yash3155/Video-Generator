### 1. Price, Volume, and Open Interest Hierarchy

* **Three-Dimensional Analytical Framework**: Technical analysis in financial markets evaluates three primary data streams: **price**, **volume**, and **open interest** [1, 2].
* **Hierarchy of Indicators**:
  1. **Price (Primary Indicator)**: Price action is by far the most critical data point [3]. All economic and fundamental forces of supply and demand discount directly into price [3, 4].
  2. **Volume (First Secondary Indicator)**: Serves as a secondary confirming indicator [3]. It is more important than open interest because volume applies to **all financial markets** (equities, futures, options, indices) [1, 3, 5, 6].
  3. **Open Interest (Second Secondary Indicator)**: Serves as a secondary confirming tool applied **primarily to futures and options markets** [1, 3, 5, 6]. 

#### Core Definitions
* **Price**: The recorded settlement or transaction value of an asset over a given timeframe [2, 7].
* **Volume**: The total number of units, shares, or futures contracts traded during a specified period (e.g., daily volume) [8, 9].
* **Open Interest**: The total number of outstanding or unliquidated futures or options contracts at the close of the trading day [10, 11].

#### Mechanics of Open Interest Changes
In futures markets, every contract requires both a buyer (long) and a seller (short) [11]. Changes in open interest track the net flow of capital into or out of the market [12]:

| Buyer Action | Seller Action | Net Change in Open Interest | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Buys new position | Sells new position | **Increases (+1)** | New money entering the market [12]. |
| Buys new position | Liquidates old long | **Unchanged (0)** | Position transfer between participants [12]. |
| Liquidates old short | Sells new position | **Unchanged (0)** | Position transfer between participants [12]. |
| Liquidates old short | Liquidates old long | **Decreases (-1)** | Money leaving the market [12]. |

#### Futures Reporting Conventions & Limitations
* **Reporting Lag**: Official volume and open interest numbers in futures are reported with a **one-day lag** (published the following day) [10, 13]. Only estimated volume is available at the close of trading [10, 13]. Stock volume, by contrast, is available immediately in real time [13, 14].
* **Total vs. Individual Contract Figures**: Futures contract months experience artificial volume/open interest expansions at inception and collapses prior to expiration due to contract rollover and liquidation [15]. Therefore, technicians use **total volume and total open interest across all delivery months** for forecasting market direction [1, 15, 16].

### 2. Volume Confirming Price Trends

#### Core Rule
**Volume must expand in the direction of the major price trend** [17-19]. Volume measures the intensity, urgency, and underlying buying or selling pressure behind a price move [19, 20].

* **Uptrend Confirmation**: Volume should **increase on rallies** (up-days) and **decrease/contract on price dips** (down-days) [17, 19].
* **Downtrend Confirmation**: Volume should **increase on price declines** (down-days) and **decrease/contract on rally attempts** (up-days) [17, 21].

#### Price–Volume Relationship Matrix

| Price Trend | Volume Trend | Market Condition & Confirmation Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rising** | **Increasing** | **Strong / Bullish Confirmation**: Aggressive buying pressure pushing prices higher [18, 19]. |
| **Rising** | **Decreasing** | **Weak / Bearish Divergence**: Buying pressure is drying up; uptrend is vulnerable [18, 22]. |
| **Declining** | **Increasing** | **Weak Market / Bearish Confirmation**: Aggressive supply and selling pressure [18, 21]. |
| **Declining** | **Decreasing** | **Strong Market / Bullish Signal**: Selling pressure is diminishing; downside move is a temporary correction [18, 21]. |

#### Volume Rules Across Chart Formations

##### Reversal Patterns
* **Upside Breakouts**: Volume expansion is **absolutely essential** for valid upside breakouts from basing patterns or bottoms [23-26]. If volume fails to expand on an upside breakout, the pattern is suspect [24].
* **Market Tops vs. Market Bottoms**: Top reversal patterns can "fall of their own weight" without heavy initial volume as supply overwhelms demand [24, 25]. Bottom reversals, however, require a heavy influx of buying pressure (high volume) to launch a new bull market [24-26].
* **Head & Shoulders Top**: Volume is heaviest on the left shoulder, diminishes on the head, is noticeably lighter on the right shoulder, and expands sharply on the neckline breakdown [27, 28].

##### Continuation Patterns
* **Triangles, Flags, and Pennants**: Trading volume gradually diminishes as price consolidates within the pattern [21, 29, 30]. The resolution/breakout must be accompanied by a dramatic surge in volume [21, 29, 31].

##### Market Climaxes
* **Blowoffs**: Occur at market tops when prices suddenly rally sharply after a long advance, accompanied by a massive jump in trading activity, followed by an abrupt top [32].
* **Selling Climaxes**: Occur at market bottoms characterized by a sudden, violent downside price plunge on unusually heavy volume, followed by a rapid rebound [32, 33].

### 3. "Volume Precedes Price"

* **Concept**: Price and volume both measure market pressure [20, 34]. Price reflects the direction of momentum, while volume reflects the underlying force driving it [20, 34].
* **Rule**: Technical analysts operate on the principle that **volume precedes price** [5, 34]. This means that shifts in buying or selling pressure—such as the loss of momentum or subtle accumulation/distribution—are reflected in volume figures **before** they manifest as visible price trend reversals [5, 34].

### 4. On Balance Volume (OBV)

#### Origin & Purpose
Developed and popularized by **Joseph Granville** in his 1963 book *Granville's New Key to Stock Market Profits* [35]. OBV constructs a continuous cumulative volume line to quantify whether volume is flowing into or out of a security [35, 36].

#### Mathematical Formula & Calculation Rules
OBV assigns total daily volume a positive or negative value based on whether the closing price is higher or lower than the previous day's close [36].

\\[\text{OBV}_t = \begin{cases} \text{OBV}_{t-1} + V_t & \text{if } P_t > P_{t-1} \\[6pt] \text{OBV}_{t-1} - V_t & \text{if } P_t < P_{t-1} \\[6pt] \text{OBV}_{t-1} & \text{if } P_t = P_{t-1} \end{cases}\\] [36]

**Variables**:
* \\(\text{OBV}_t\\): On Balance Volume total for current period \\(t\\) [36].
* \\(\text{OBV}_{t-1}\\): On Balance Volume total for prior period \\(t-1\\) [36].
* \\(V_t\\): Total trading volume for period \\(t\\) [36].
* \\(P_t\\): Closing price for period \\(t\\) [36].
* \\(P_{t-1}\\): Closing price for prior period \\(t-1\\) [36].

#### Interpretation & Trading Rules
* **Direction Over Absolute Values**: The absolute numerical value of OBV is irrelevant; analysts must evaluate only the **slope and direction (trend) of the OBV line** [37].
* **Trend Confirmation**: In an uptrend, the OBV line should exhibit a series of higher peaks and higher troughs [37, 38]. In a downtrend, the OBV line should exhibit lower peaks and lower troughs [37, 38].

---

### 5. OBV Divergences

#### Bearish Divergence
* **Rule**: Occurs when price moves into new highs (or forms a higher peak), but the OBV line fails to break its previous peak or trends downward [22, 38, 39].
* **Implication**: Signals that upside volume pressure is waning despite higher nominal prices, providing an early warning of an impending market top or downward trend reversal [22, 38, 39].

#### Bullish Divergence
* **Rule**: Occurs when price moves into new lows (or forms a lower trough), but the OBV line forms a higher trough or fails to break its prior low [38, 40].
* **Implication**: Indicates that downside volume pressure is drying up, providing an early warning of an impending market bottom or upward trend reversal [38, 40].

#### OBV Breakout Confirmation & Leading Signals
* **OBV Trendline Breakouts**: Trendlines drawn directly across the peaks or troughs of the OBV line often break out **prior to price trendline breakouts**, acting as a leading indicator for upcoming price moves [40, 41].

### 6. OBV Variations & Advanced Volume Indicators

#### Limitations of Standard OBV
1. **Binary Volume Allocation**: Assigns 100% of a day's volume a positive or negative sign even if the market closes up or down by only one tick, or if a market spent all day rallying but closed slightly down [38].
2. **Futures Limit-Day Distortion**: Locked limit-up days in futures feature extreme buying demand but report very light volume due to a lack of sellers, artificially depressing OBV readings [39].

#### Key Variations & Alternative Volume Indicators

##### 1. Weighted OBV (Price-Weighted Volume)
* **Concept**: Multiplies daily volume by the magnitude of price change (or percentage price change) [42].
* **Purpose**: Gives greater weight to days with large price moves and reduces the influence of days with minimal price change [42].

##### 2. Real-Time Money Flow (Laszlo Birinyi, Jr.)
* **Concept**: Applies OBV logic to real-time intraday tick data, evaluating volume on every single price tick [14].
* **Purpose**: Tracks whether capital is flowing into or out of a security throughout the trading day [14].

##### 3. Demand Index (DI - James Sibbet)
* **Concept**: A complex oscillator combining price and volume into a ratio of **Buying Pressure (BP)** to **Selling Pressure (SP)** [14, 43, 44].
* **Formula**:
  \\[\text{DI} = \frac{\text{BP}}{\text{SP}} \quad (\text{or } \frac{\text{SP}}{\text{BP}} \text{ when } \text{BP} > \text{SP})\\] [44, 45]
  * On up-days: \\(\text{BP} = V\\), \\(\text{SP} = \frac{V}{P}\\) [45].
  * On down-days: \\(\text{BP} = \frac{V}{P}\\), \\(\text{SP} = V\\) [45].
  * \\(P\\) is the percentage price change modified by volatility constant \\(K = \frac{3 \times C}{\text{VA}}\\) (where \\(C\\) is close and \\(\text{VA}\\) is the 10-day average price range) [45].

##### 4. Herrick Payoff Index (HPI - John Herrick)
* **Concept**: Combines price action, volume, and open interest to track money flow in futures markets [14, 46, 47].
* **Interpretation**: Values above zero indicate net capital inflows (bullish), while values below zero indicate net capital outflows (bearish) [47].

##### 5. Equivolume Charting (Richard Arms) & CandlePower (Greg Morris)
* **Equivolume**: Replaces standard bar lines with boxes where **vertical height represents the price range** and **horizontal width represents daily volume** [48, 49].
* **CandlePower / Candlevolume**: Blends Japanese Candlestick bodies with Equivolume variable box widths [50].

### 7. Author Case Studies (John J. Murphy)

#### Intel Corp (1997 Bearish OBV Divergence Case Study)
* **Setup**: In mid-1997, Intel's stock price rallied strongly, reaching new highs near the \$100 level in August 1997 [105, Figure 7.7].
* **Analysis**: During this price rally, the On Balance Volume (OBV) line failed to make a new high and displayed a distinct downward trend (bearish divergence) [105, Figure 7.7].
* **Outcome**: The OBV bearish divergence correctly forecasted a major trend reversal, which was followed by a severe price decline in Intel down toward \$66 [105, Figure 7.7].

```
Intel Price ($)     [ Peak ~100 ] -------------> [ Major Collapse to ~66 ]
                     /
OBV Line            [ Lower Peak ] \ (Diverging Down)
```

#### Compaq Computer Corp (1997 Volume & OBV Breakdown Case Study)
* **Setup**: In October 1997, Compaq reached a major top [96, 100, Figures 7.5 & 7.6].
* **Analysis**: A sudden downside price move was accompanied by an explosive jump in daily volume bars (heavy downside pressure) [96, Figure 7.5]. Simultaneously, the OBV line broke below a prominent upward trendline [100, Figure 7.6].
* **Outcome**: Confirmed a major trend reversal from bull to bear, leading to a prolonged price decline [96, 100, Figures 7.5 & 7.6].

#### McDonald’s Corp (1997–1998 OBV Leading Indicator Case Study)
* **Setup**: McDonald’s traded in a horizontal consolidation range through late 1997 [92, 106, Figures 7.3 & 7.8].
* **Analysis**: When OBV was overlaid directly on top of the price bars, the OBV line began making higher lows and trending upward **ahead of price** [106, Figure 7.8].
* **Outcome**: The leading OBV breakout provided advance confirmation of the subsequent bullish price breakout through resistance at \$48–\$52 in early 1998 [92, 106, Figures 7.3 & 7.8].

#### Futures Market Open Interest Studies (Silver, Gold, Copper, Crude Oil 1997–1998)
* **Silver Futures (1997–1998)**: Price rally accompanied by steadily rising open interest confirmed aggressive new long buying (bullish confirmation) [107, Figure 7.9].
* **Gold Futures (1997–1998)**: Price rally accompanied by declining open interest signaled short covering rather than new buying, correctly warning that the rally would fail [108, 109, Figure 7.10].
* **Copper Futures (1997–1998)**: Price breakdown accompanied by expanding open interest confirmed aggressive short selling (bearish confirmation) [110, Figure 7.11].

Developed and popularized by Joseph Granville in his 1963 book, Granville's New Key to Stock Market Profits, OBV actually produces a curving line on the price chart. A higher close causes the volume for that day to be given a plus value, while a lower close counts for negative volume. A running cumulative total is then maintained by adding or subtracting each day's volume based on the direction of the market close."
