### Flags and Pennants ("Flying at Half-Mast")

* **Core Concepts & Definitions**: Flags and pennants are brief pauses or consolidation phases in a dynamic, steep market move [1, 2]. They represent situations where an advance or decline has gotten ahead of itself, allowing the market to catch its breath before resuming the prevailing trend [2]. Both formations are highly reliable continuation patterns and rarely result in trend reversals [2].
* **Chart Patterns & Construction**:
  * **Flagpole**: Both patterns are preceded by a steep, almost straight-line move on heavy volume, known as the flagpole [2-4].
  * **Flag**: Resembles a small parallelogram bounded by two parallel trendlines that slope *against* the prevailing trend (e.g., sloping downward in an uptrend or upward in a downtrend) [3, 4].
  * **Pennant**: Bounded by two converging trendlines and is more horizontal, closely resembling a small symmetrical triangle [4, 5].
* **Duration**: Short-term patterns that complete within **1 to 3 weeks** in uptrends, and often **1 to 2 weeks** in downtrends [4, 6].
* **Volume Rules**: Heavy volume during the initial flagpole move; a dramatic drop-off ("drying up") of volume while the pattern forms; and a sudden burst of heavy trading volume on the breakout [2, 4-6]. Volume confirmation is especially critical on upside breakouts [6].
* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * **Flagpole Height (\\(H_{\text{flagpole}}\\))**: \\(H_{\text{flagpole}} = |P_{\text{flagpole\_peak}} - P_{\text{initial\_breakout}}|\\) where \\(P_{\text{initial\_breakout}}\\) is the price level where the original trend signal/breakout occurred [7].
  * **Price Target Formula**: \\(P_{\text{target}} = P_{\text{flag\_breakout}} \pm H_{\text{flagpole}}\\) (add \\(H_{\text{flagpole}}\\) for upside breakouts; subtract for downside breakdowns) [7].
  * **"Half-Mast" Principle**: The pattern tends to appear near the halfway point of the entire market move, meaning the move following the breakout will duplicate the size of the move preceding it [4, 7, 8].
* **Trading Rules**: Buy upon a decisive closing penetration of the upper trendline in an uptrend; sell short upon penetration of the lower trendline in a downtrend [6, 7].

### Rising and Falling Wedges

* **Core Concepts & Definitions**: Wedges are intermediate consolidation patterns formed by two converging trendlines that meet at an apex, similar to symmetrical triangles, but distinguished by a pronounced slant [9].
* **Chart Patterns & Slopes**:
  * **Falling Wedge**: Formed by two downward-sloping converging trendlines. Because it slants against an uptrend, it is **bullish** [9, 10].
  * **Rising Wedge**: Formed by two upward-sloping converging trendlines. Because it slants against a downtrend, it is **bearish** [9-11].
  * **Reversal Exceptions**: When a rising wedge occurs near the end of an uptrend, or a falling wedge occurs at the end of a downtrend, they act as major top or bottom reversal patterns [10]. Regardless of location, the fundamental maxim holds: **a rising wedge is bearish and a falling wedge is bullish** [10, 12].
* **Duration**: Intermediate term, lasting **more than 1 month but no more than 3 months** [9].
* **Volume Rules**: Volume diminishes gradually as the price action moves toward the apex, followed by a noticeable volume increase on the breakout [9, 13, 14].
* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * **Base Height (\\(H_{\text{base}}\\))**: \\(H_{\text{base}} = |P_{\text{high\_base}} - P_{\text{low\_base}}|\\) measured at the widest part of the pattern base [9, 15].
  * **Price Target Formula**: \\(P_{\text{target}} = P_{\text{breakout}} \pm H_{\text{base}}\\) [9, 15].
* **Trading Rules**: Enter positions in the direction of the expected breakout (go long on upside breaks of falling wedges; sell short on downside breaks of rising wedges) [9, 10, 14].

### Rectangle Trading Ranges

* **Core Concepts & Definitions**: Rectangles (also called trading ranges, congestion areas, or "lines" in Dow Theory) represent sideways consolidation pauses where price moves laterally between two parallel horizontal lines [11, 12, 16, 17]. 
* **Chart Pattern & Structure**: Bounded by a flat horizontal upper line (resistance) and a flat horizontal lower line (support) [11, 12, 17].
* **Duration**: Intermediate term, usually lasting **1 to 3 months** [18].
* **Volume Rules & Confirmation**: Unlike other continuation patterns where activity dries up, the wide price swings inside a rectangle maintain trading activity [18, 19].
  * **Volume Bias**: Heavier volume on up-bounces than down-dips signals bullish continuation; heavier volume on downside moves signals potential topping/reversal [19]. Breakouts must be accompanied by expanding volume [20].
* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * **Rectangle Height (\\(H_{\text{rect}}\\))**: \\(H_{\text{rect}} = P_{\text{resistance}} - P_{\text{support}}\\) [20].
  * **Price Target Formula**: \\(P_{\text{target}} = P_{\text{breakout}} \pm H_{\text{rect}}\\) [20].
* **Trading Rules & Support/Resistance Role Reversal**:
  * **Range Traders**: Buy near lower support band in uptrends; sell/short near upper resistance band in downtrends [18].
  * **Breakout Traders**: Wait for a decisive close outside the upper or lower boundary [17, 18].
  * **Role Reversal**: Upon an upside breakout, the former resistance ceiling (\\(P_{\text{resistance}}\\)) becomes a support floor on pullbacks; upon a downside breakdown, former support (\\(P_{\text{support}}\\)) becomes resistance [20].

### The Measured Move (Swing Measurement)

* **Core Concepts & Definitions**: Describes a market phenomenon where a major advance or decline splits into two equal and parallel move segments separated by an orderly countertrend correction [21, 22].
* **Chart Pattern & Structure**:
  * **Leg 1 (\\(AB\\))**: The initial well-defined trend leg from point \\(A\\) to point \\(B\\) [21, 22].
  * **Corrective Wave (\\(BC\\))**: A countertrend reaction that retraces **\\(33\%\\) to \\(50\%\\) (\\(1/3\\) to \\(1/2\\))** of wave \\(AB\\) [21-23].
  * **Leg 2 (\\(CD\\))**: The second trend leg that duplicates the vertical distance and slope of wave \\(AB\\) [21-23].
* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * **First Leg Height (\\(H_{AB}\\))**: \\(H_{AB} = |P_B - P_A|\\) [21, 23].
  * **Measured Move Target (\\(P_D\\))**: \\(P_D = P_C + H_{AB}\\) for uptrends (or \\(P_D = P_C - H_{AB}\\) for downtrends), projected directly from the corrective low/high point \\(C\\) [21, 23].
* **Author Case Study**:
  * **Procter & Gamble Co. (PG)** daily bar chart: Prior upleg \\(AB\\) measured \\(20\\) points (\\(A \to B\\)) [21]. The countertrend correction dropped to a low point at \\(C = 62\\) [21]. Adding the \\(20\\)-point move of leg \\(AB\\) to point \\(C\\) (\\(62 + 20\\)) yielded an exact measured price target of \\(D = 82\\) [21].

### Continuation Head and Shoulders

* **Core Concepts & Definitions**: A consolidation variation of the classic Head and Shoulders pattern that develops mid-trend and appears turned upside down relative to what would be expected for a trend reversal at that position [23-25].
* **Chart Pattern & Structure**:
  * **Bullish Continuation H&S (in an Uptrend)**: Resembles an inverse Head and Shoulders pattern, where the middle trough (head) dips lower than the left and right reaction lows (shoulders) [25, 26]. A decisive close above the neckline confirms pattern completion and resumes the uptrend [25-27].
  * **Bearish Continuation H&S (in a Downtrend)**: Resembles an upright Head and Shoulders top, where the middle peak (head) stands higher than the surrounding shoulder peaks [25, 26]. A close below the neckline resumes the downtrend [25, 26].
* **Volume Rules**: Volume patterns match standard Head and Shoulders formations: volume should diminish on the head and right shoulder, drying up prior to the breakout, followed by a sharp expansion of trading activity as prices penetrate the neckline [28-31].
* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * **Pattern Height (\\(H_{\text{head}}\\))**: \\(H_{\text{head}} = |P_{\text{head}} - P_{\text{neckline}}|\\) measured vertically from the head peak/trough to the neckline [32, 33].
  * **Price Target Formula**: \\(P_{\text{target}} = P_{\text{neckline\_breakout}} \pm H_{\text{head}}\\) [32, 33].
* **Author Case Study**:
  * **General Motors Corp (GM)** daily bar chart (first half of 1997): GM formed a clear bullish continuation Head and Shoulders pattern within an existing uptrend [26, 27]. The pattern was completed, resuming the major uptrend, when prices closed decisively above the horizontal neckline at \\(60\\) [26, 27].

### Confirmation vs. Divergence

* **Core Definitions**:
  * **Confirmation**: The comparison of all technical indicators, signals, volume, and related market averages to verify that they are pointing in the same direction and validating price action [27, 34].
  * **Divergence**: A situation where technical indicators fail to confirm price action (e.g., price reaches new highs while volume or oscillators fail to do so), serving as a primary early warning signal of impending trend reversal [27, 35-37].
* **Confirmation & Divergence Rules by Category**:

| Indicator / Tool | Confirmation Signal | Divergence / Failure Warning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Volume Analysis** | Volume expands in the direction of the main price trend (increases on rallies in uptrend; increases on selloffs in downtrend) [38-41]. | New price high occurs on declining volume, or volume expands on countertrend dips [36, 41]. |
| **Open Interest (Futures)** | Price \\(\uparrow\\) + Open Interest \\(\uparrow\\) = **Bullish / Strong** [39, 42, 43].<br>Price \\(\downarrow\\) + Open Interest \\(\uparrow\\) = **Bearish / Weak** (Aggressive shorting) [39, 43]. | Price \\(\uparrow\\) + Open Interest \\(\downarrow\\) = **Bearish / Weak** (Short covering) [39, 43].<br>Price \\(\downarrow\\) + Open Interest \\(\downarrow\\) = **Bullish / Strong** (Long liquidation) [39, 43]. |
| **Oscillators (Momentum, ROC, RSI, Stochastics, MACD)** | Oscillator makes new highs/lows in tandem with price setting new highs/lows [44-47]. | **Bearish Divergence**: Price sets a higher high while oscillator sets a lower high in overbought territory (\\(>80\\) or \\(>70\\)) [36, 45, 46].<br>**Bullish Divergence**: Price sets a lower low while oscillator sets a higher low in oversold territory (\\(<20\\) or \\(<30\\)) [45, 46]. |
| **Dow Theory & Market Breadth** | Major averages (Dow Industrials & Transports) hit new highs together [48]. | One market average hits a new high while the other fails to confirm [48]. |

* **Mathematical Indicator Formulas**:
  * **10-Day Momentum**: \\(M = V - V_x\\) (where \\(V\\) is the latest close and \\(V_x\\) is the close \\(x\\) days ago) [49].
  * **10-Day Rate of Change (ROC)**: \\(\text{ROC} = 100 \times \left( \frac{V}{V_x} \right)\\) [50].
  * **Stochastics (\\(\%K\\))**: \\(\%K = 100 \times \left[ \frac{C - L_{14}}{H_{14} - L_{14}} \right]\\) (where \\(C\\) is latest close, \\(L_{14}\\) is lowest low, \\(H_{14}\\) is highest high over 14 periods) [51].
* **Trading Rules for Divergence**: Divergence alone is a warning light, not an immediate stand-alone sell or buy trigger; traders should wait for price confirmation (such as a trendline break, support/resistance violation, or oscillator zero-line/moving average crossover) before initiating trades [45, 47, 52, 53].

The vertical distance, therefore, would be the difference, which is 20. That 20 points would be measured downward from the level at which the neckline is broken. If the neckline in Figure 5.1a is at 82 when broken, a downside objective would be projected to the 62 level (82 - 20 = 62)."

- "MEASURING MOMENTUM The concept of momentum is the most basic application of oscillator analysis. Momentum measures the velocity of price changes as opposed to the actual price levels themselves. Market momentum is measured by continually taking price differences for a fixed time interval. To construct a 10 day momentum line, simply subtract the closing price 10 days ago from the last closing price. This positive or negative value is then plotted around a zero line. The formula for momentum is: M = V - Vx"
