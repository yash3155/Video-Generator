### 1. Principal-Protected Notes (PPNs)

* **Definition & Structure**: A Principal-Protected Note is a structured financial product created by combining a **zero-coupon bond** paying face value at maturity with a **call option** (or portfolio of call options) on an underlying asset or index [5, 6].
* **Creation Mechanics**:
  * An issuer takes a portion of the capital (e.g., \$600,000 out of a \$1,000,000 pool or 60 cents on the dollar) to purchase zero-coupon government bonds maturing at full face value (\$1,000,000) at the end of a specified term (e.g., 7 years) [5].
  * The remaining capital (\$400,000) is used to buy at-the-money call options on the underlying stock or market index [5, 7].
* **Conditions for Creation**:
  * PPNs require a sufficient gap between the discounted purchase price of the zero-coupon bond and its face value to fund the required option premiums [5].
  * Higher interest rates lower the present value of the zero-coupon bond, leaving more funds to purchase upside call options [5].
* **Payoff Profile**:
  * **Downside**: If the underlying asset falls or remains flat, the call options expire worthless, but the zero-coupon bond matures at 100% of face value, guaranteeing that the investor receives back their initial principal [5, 8].
  * **Upside**: If the underlying asset appreciates, the call options gain value, providing market participation on top of the guaranteed principal [5, 7].
  * **Bull Spread Variation**: Issuers can also cap the maximum payoff by using a bull call spread instead of an outright call option, lowering option costs [9, 10].

### 2. Covered Calls and Protective Puts

#### Covered Call
* **Definition**: A strategy consisting of a **long stock position** combined with a **short call option** on the same stock [11, 12].
* **Payoff Profile**:
  * Provides limited downside protection (equal to the call premium received) while capping upside profit at the option's strike price [11, 12].
* **Formulas**:
  * \\(\text{Downside Break-Even} = \text{Stock Purchase Price} - \text{Call Premium Received}\\) [13, 14].
  * \\(\text{Maximum Profit} = (\text{Call Strike Price} - \text{Stock Purchase Price}) + \text{Call Premium Received}\\) [14, 15].
* **Numerical Example**:
  * Buy stock at \$49 and sell a call with strike \$50 for \$6 [14].
  * \\(\text{Maximum Profit} = (50 - 49) + 6 = 7\\) points (\$700) if stock is \\(\ge 50\\) at expiration [14].
  * \\(\text{Downside Break-Even} = 49 - 6 = 43\\) [14].

#### Protective Put (Married Put)
* **Definition**: A strategy consisting of a **long stock position** combined with a **long put option** on the same stock [16-18].
* **Payoff Profile**:
  * Caps maximum loss below the put strike price while maintaining uncapped upside potential as the stock price rises [16, 17].
* **Formulas**:
  * \\(\text{Break-Even Price} = \text{Stock Purchase Price} + \text{Put Premium Paid}\\) [17, 19].
  * \\(\text{Maximum Loss} = (\text{Stock Purchase Price} - \text{Put Strike Price}) + \text{Put Premium Paid}\\) [19].
* **Numerical Example**:
  * Stock purchased at \$50 combined with a long put at strike \$50 costing \$4 [17].
  * \\(\text{Maximum Loss} = (50 - 50) + 4 = 4\\) points (\$400) if stock falls below \$50 [17].
  * \\(\text{Break-Even Price} = 50 + 4 = 54\\) [17].

### 5. Box Spreads

* **Definition & Structure**: A Box Spread is formed by combining a **bull call spread** (buy call \\(K_1\\), sell call \\(K_2\\)) and a **bear put spread** (buy put \\(K_2\\), sell put \\(K_1\\)) using the same strike prices \\(K_1 < K_2\\) and expiration date [4, 42].
* **Payoff**: At expiration, exactly one spread will be in the money with a guaranteed payoff of \\(K_2 - K_1\\) regardless of stock price \\(S_T\\) [4].
* **No-Arbitrage Present Value Rule**:
  * In an arbitrage-free market, the initial net debit paid to purchase the box spread must equal the present value of \\(K_2 - K_1\\) discounted at the risk-free rate [43]:
    \\[\text{Box Price} = (K_2 - K_1) e^{-r T}\\]
  * If the market price is less than \\((K_2 - K_1) e^{-r T}\\), an arbitrageur can buy the box spread to lock in a risk-free return above the risk-free rate [4, 43].
* **Numerical Example**:
  * Stock at \\(55\\). Strikes \\(K_1 = 50\\), \\(K_2 = 60\\) (\\(K_2 - K_1 = 10\\)).
  * Call bull spread: Buy 50 call for \\(7\\), sell 60 call for \\(2\\) (\\(\text{Net Cost} = 5\\) debit) [4].
  * Put bear spread: Buy 60 put for \\(5.50\\), sell 50 put for \\(1\\) (\\(\text{Net Cost} = 4.50\\) debit) [4].
  * Total box cost = \\(5 + 4.50 = 9.50\\) debit [4].
  * Payoff at expiration = \\(10\\) [4]. Arbitrage profit = \\(10 - 9.50 = 0.50\\) points (\$50) minus interest/carrying costs [4, 43].

### 6. Butterfly Spreads

* **Definition & Structure**: Consists of **buying one call at lower strike \\(K_1\\)**, **buying one call at higher strike \\(K_3\\)**, and **selling two calls at middle strike \\(K_2\\)**, where \\(K_2 = \frac{K_1 + K_3}{2}\\) [3, 44, 45]. Always requires a net debit [3, 46].
* **Payoff Profile**:
  * \\(S_T \le K_1\\) or \\(S_T \ge K_3\\): All options expire worthless or offset; maximum loss equals the net debit [46, 47].
  * \\(K_1 < S_T \le K_2\\): Payoff equals \\((S_T - K_1) - \text{Net Debit}\\) [46, 47].
  * \\(K_2 < S_T < K_3\\): Payoff equals \\((K_3 - S_T) - \text{Net Debit}\\) [46, 47].
  * \\(S_T = K_2\\): Maximum profit equals \\((K_2 - K_1) - \text{Net Debit}\\) [3, 46].
  * \\(\text{Downside Break-Even} = K_1 + \text{Net Debit}\\); \\(\text{Upside Break-Even} = K_3 - \text{Net Debit}\\) [46, 47].
* **Numerical Example**:
  * Buy July 50 call for \\(12\\), sell two July 60 calls for \\(6\\) each (\\(12\\) total credit), buy July 70 call for \\(3\\) [3].
  * Strikes: \\(K_1 = 50\\), \\(K_2 = 60\\), \\(K_3 = 70\\) [3, 46].
  * \\(\text{Net Debit} = 12 - 12 + 3 = 3\\) points (\$300) [3, 46].
  * \\(\text{Maximum Profit} = (60 - 50) - 3 = 7\\) points (\$700) at \\(S_T = 60\\) [3, 46].
  * \\(\text{Downside Break-Even} = 50 + 3 = 53\\); \\(\text{Upside Break-Even} = 70 - 3 = 67\\) [46, 47].
  * \\(\text{Maximum Loss} = 3\\) points (\$300) if \\(S_T \le 50\\) or \\(S_T \ge 70\\) [47].
