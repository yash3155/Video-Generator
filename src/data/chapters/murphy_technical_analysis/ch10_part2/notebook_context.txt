### 1. Stochastics (%K and %D)

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Definition & Origin**: Popularized by George Lane, the Stochastic oscillator is based on the observation that as prices increase, daily closing prices tend to accumulate closer to the upper end of the price range [1, 2]. Conversely, in downtrends, closing prices tend to cluster near the bottom of the price range [1].
* **Fast vs. Slow Stochastics**: The indicator plots two lines—**%K** (the faster, more sensitive line) and **%D** (the slower line, which represents a smoothed moving average of %K) [1, 3]. **Fast Stochastics** uses raw %K and its 3-period moving average (%D) [3]. **Slow Stochastics** applies an additional 3-period moving average to %D to produce smoother, more reliable trading signals [3].

#### Mathematical Formulas & Variables
* **%K Line Formula**:
  \\[\%K = 100 \times \left[ \frac{C - L_{14}}{H_{14} - L_{14}} \right]\\] [4]
  * \\(C\\): The latest closing price [4].
  * \\(L_{14}\\): The lowest price low recorded during the selected time period (default: 14 days, weeks, or months) [4].
  * \\(H_{14}\\): The highest price high recorded during the same 14-period window [4].
* **%D Line Formula**:
  \\[\%D = \text{3-period moving average of } \%K\\] [3]
* **Slow Stochastics Formula**:
  \\[\text{Slow } \%K = \%D \text{ of Fast Stochastics}\\] [3]
  \\[\text{Slow } \%D = \text{3-period moving average of Slow } \%K\\] [3]

#### Chart Patterns
* **Bearish Divergence**: Occurs when the %D line is above 80 (overbought) and forms two declining peaks while the price continues to make higher highs [5].
* **Bullish Divergence**: Occurs when the %D line is below 20 (oversold) and forms two rising troughs while the price continues to make lower lows [5].
* **Double Tops/Bottoms**: Double tops or bottoms forming on the %D line within extreme territory serve as high-conviction pattern setups [6].

#### Confirmation Rules
* **Extreme Thresholds**: Readings above **80** indicate an **overbought** condition (closing prices near the top of the range), while readings below **20** indicate an **oversold** condition [3, 5].
* **Setup/Alert**: A trading setup is established only when the %D line enters an extreme area (over 80 or under 20) and shows divergence against price action [5, 6].

#### Trading Rules
1. **Buy Signal**: Triggered when the faster %K line crosses **above** the slower %D line while both lines are below the **20** oversold threshold [5, 7].
2. **Sell Signal**: Triggered when the faster %K line crosses **below** the slower %D line while both lines are above the **80** overbought threshold [5, 7].
3. **Candlestick Pattern Filtering**: Under Greg Morris’s filtered candle pattern rules, reversal candlestick patterns (e.g., Piercing Line, Dark Cloud Cover) are only valid if %D is in its **presignal area** (below 20 for buys or above 80 for sells); patterns occurring at mid-level readings (e.g., 65) are ignored [8, 9].

#### Author Case Studies & Examples
* **Treasury Bond Futures**: Weekly bar charts of T-Bonds demonstrate clear buy signals when %K crosses above %D under 20 and sell signals when %K crosses below %D over 80 [7, 10].
* **3Com Corp (Filtered Candlesticks)**: A Piercing Line reversal pattern was confirmed because it coincided directly with Stochastic %D entering the pre-signal zone below 20 [8].
* **Intraday Timing**: 10-minute and 60-minute Stochastics charts on Deutsche Mark and T-Bond futures are highlighted for fine-tuning intraday execution [11, 12].

### 2. Larry Williams %R

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Definition**: Developed by Larry Williams, **%R** measures the position of the current closing price relative to the total price range over a specified number of days [13].
* **Inverted Calculation**: Because %R measures how far the close is from the high of the period, raw %R moves in an inverse direction compared to standard oscillators [13]. Charting platforms invert the scale so that higher values plot at the top and lower values at the bottom, matching standard Stochastic displays [13].

#### Mathematical Formulas & Variables
* **Formula**:
  \\[\%R = \frac{H_n - C}{H_n - L_n} \times 100\\] [13]
  *(Or expressed on an inverted 0 to -100 / 0 to 100 scale)* [13, 14]
  * \\(C\\): Today's / latest closing price [13].
  * \\(H_n\\): The highest price high over the selected \\(n\\)-day period (default: 20 trading days, reflecting the 28-calendar-day monthly cycle) [13-15].
  * \\(L_n\\): The lowest price low over the same \\(n\\)-day period [13].

#### Chart Patterns
* **Bullish/Bearish Divergence**: Just like Stochastics, the primary structural patterns to look for are momentum divergences in extreme territories—where prices push to a new high or low, but %R fails to register a corresponding peak or trough [13].

#### Confirmation Rules
* **Overbought Threshold**: Readings **above 80** (or 80–100 depending on platform scaling) signal an overbought market [14].
* **Oversold Threshold**: Readings **below 20** signal an oversold market [14].

#### Trading Rules
1. **Trend Alignment**: Never trade %R overbought/oversold readings against the primary market trend [16].
2. **Execution**: Look for buying opportunities when %R enters the oversold zone (<20) during an established uptrend, and sell short when %R enters the overbought zone (>80) during an established downtrend [13, 16].

#### Author Case Studies & Examples
* **S&P 500 Stock Index**: A 20-day Williams %R oscillator plotted against daily price action demonstrates how extreme readings (>80 and <20) pinpoint intermediate price turnarounds [14].

### 3. When Oscillators Are Most Useful

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Subordinate Role**: Oscillators are **secondary indicators** that must always be subordinated to basic trend analysis [16, 17]. Their primary functions are measuring momentum velocity, identifying overbought/oversold extremes, and warning of trend fatigue via divergence [18, 19].

#### Optimal Market Environments (When Most Useful)
1. **Trading Ranges / Non-Trending Markets**: Oscillators perform at their peak when markets move sideways in horizontal price bands [20, 21]. In these phases, trend-following tools (like moving averages) suffer continuous whipsaws, whereas oscillators fluctuate predictably between upper and lower boundaries [20, 22].
2. **Maturing Trends / Trend Endings**: Oscillators become invaluable toward the end of an extended move by displaying divergence or failing to confirm new price highs/lows, alerting traders to an impending trend reversal [17, 23].

#### Ineffective Market Environments (When LEAST Useful & The Breakout Dilemma)
* **Early Stages of Major Breakouts**: When a market breaks out of a multi-month trading range to initiate a powerful new bull or bear trend, the oscillator immediately surges into an extreme overbought or oversold territory [24].
* **The Trader's Dilemma**: Traders often mistakenly sell an overbought reading or avoid buying a bullish breakout because the oscillator is overbought [24]. In strong new trends, overbought markets can stay overbought for extended periods [23, 25].
* **Rule for Early Breakouts**: **Ignore the oscillator during the early stages of a strong breakout** and rely strictly on basic trend analysis [23]. Reintroduce oscillator weight only as the trend matures [23].

#### Trend Filters for Oscillator Selection
* **Wilder’s ADX Filter**: The **Average Directional Index (ADX)** determines whether a market is in a trending or trading mode [26, 27].
  * **Falling ADX (below 20)**: Market is non-trending / sideways \\(\rightarrow\\) **Favor Oscillators** [22, 26, 27].
  * **Rising ADX (above 20/40)**: Market is strongly trending \\(\rightarrow\\) **Favor Moving Averages / Trend-Following** [22, 26, 27].

### 4. Moving Average Convergence/Divergence (MACD) and MACD Histogram

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Definition & Origin**: Developed by Gerald Appel, MACD combines the principles of an overbought/oversold oscillator with a dual exponential moving average crossover system [28, 29].
* **MACD Histogram**: Developed to plot the net difference between the MACD Line and the Signal Line as a visual histogram centered around a zero line [30].

#### Mathematical Formulas & Variables
* **MACD Line**:
  \\[\text{MACD Line} = \text{EMA}_{12}(\text{Close}) - \text{EMA}_{26}(\text{Close})\\] [31]
  * Difference between the 12-period and 26-period Exponential Moving Averages of closing prices [31].
* **Signal Line**:
  \\[\text{Signal Line} = \text{EMA}_9(\text{MACD Line})\\] [31]
  * 9-period Exponential Moving Average of the MACD Line [31].
* **MACD Histogram**:
  \\[\text{MACD Histogram} = \text{MACD Line} - \text{Signal Line}\\] [30]

#### Chart Patterns
* **Bearish Divergence**: MACD lines are well above zero (overbought) and begin to roll over while prices push to new highs [32].
* **Bullish Divergence**: MACD lines are well below zero (oversold) and turn upward while prices push to new lows [32].
* **Trendline Breakouts**: Direct trendlines drawn across MACD peaks or troughs often break prior to price trendline breaks [32].

#### Confirmation Rules
* **Zero-Line Position**: Overbought conditions exist when MACD lines are stretched far above the zero line; oversold conditions exist when far below zero [33, 34].
* **Best Buy/Sell Environment**: The highest-probability buy signals occur when crossover signals take place **well below the zero line** (oversold) [34]. The best sell signals occur **well above the zero line** [30, 34].

#### Trading Rules
1. **MACD Line Crossovers**:
   * **Buy Signal**: Faster MACD Line crosses **above** the slower Signal Line [33].
   * **Sell Signal**: Faster MACD Line crosses **below** the slower Signal Line [33].
2. **Zero-Line Crossings**: Crossing above zero is bullish; crossing below zero is bearish [34].
3. **MACD Histogram Rules**:
   * Histogram bars above zero indicate positive MACD alignment; below zero indicates negative alignment [30].
   * **Early Warning Exit**: When the histogram is positive but begins shrinking back toward zero, upside momentum is fading [35]. When negative and shrinking toward zero, downside momentum is fading [35].
   * *Caution*: Use histogram turns for **early exit of existing positions**, not to open new positions against the primary trend [35].

#### Author Case Studies & Examples
* **Nasdaq Composite Index**: Daily charts illustrate five consecutive MACD crossover signals, highlighting a major negative divergence in October 1997 preceding a sharp decline [32-34].
* **Weekly Nasdaq MACD Histogram**: Showcases how weekly histogram turns preceded actual crossover buy/sell signals by 2 to 10 weeks [36, 37].

### 5. Combining Weeklies and Dailies

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Top-Down (Macro-to-Micro) Approach**: Technical analysis should always progress from long-term charts to short-term charts [38, 39]. Studying daily charts alone creates a narrow perspective that misses major support/resistance levels and structural trends [38, 40].

#### Hierarchy & Timeframe Sequence
1. **20-Year Monthly Bar Chart**: Evaluates secular multi-decade trends, major historic pattern boundaries, and fundamental support/resistance [38].
2. **5-Year Weekly Bar Chart**: Analyzes intermediate trends, primary moving average alignments, and dominant cycle phases [38].
3. **6 to 9-Month Daily Bar Chart**: Identifies tactical swing patterns and precise entry/exit setups [38, 39].
4. **Intraday Charts (10 to 60-Minute)**: Used for microscopic execution timing and placing tight protective stops [38, 39].

#### Trading & Confirmation Rules
* **The Golden Rule**: **Weekly signals are ALWAYS more important than daily signals** [36].
* **Weekly Trend Filter**: Use weekly charts to establish overall market direction, and daily charts to fine-tune timing [36].
* **Alignment Rule**: **Take daily buy/sell signals ONLY when they agree with the direction of the weekly chart** [36]. Never trade a daily signal that contradicts the weekly trend filter [36].

#### Specific Indicator Applications Across Timeframes
* **MACD & Stochastics**: Weekly MACD/Stochastic crossovers define whether the trader is exclusively a buyer or a seller; daily crossovers are used solely to enter in that designated direction [10, 36].
* **Moving Average Alignment**: Combine 10-week and 40-week moving averages on weekly charts to establish the primary macro trend before acting on daily moving average crossovers [41, 42].
* **Reversal Significance**: A weekly key reversal (opening past the prior week's range and closing past the prior Friday's close) carries vastly greater technical weight than a daily key reversal day [43, 44].

### 6. Principle of Contrary Opinion & Investor Sentiment Readings

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Definition**: A psychological dimension of market analysis holding that when the vast majority of market participants agree on a market's direction, they are almost always wrong [37, 45].
* **Foundational Literature**: Formulated by Humphrey B. Neill (*The Art of Contrary Thinking*, 1954) and applied to futures by James H. Sibbet (Market Vane Bullish Consensus, 1964) [45].
* **Psychological Rationale**:
  1. **Exhaustion of Buying/Selling Power**: When 80%–90% of traders are bullish, they have already committed their funds to long positions [46, 47]. With no remaining sidelined capital to buy, the market becomes overbought and must collapse under its own weight [46, 47].
  2. **Strong vs. Weak Hands**: Futures trading is zero-sum [48]. If 80% of small retail traders are long, the remaining 20% holding short positions are commercial hedgers [48]. Because 20% absorb 80% of the contract volume, the commercial shorts are heavily capitalized **strong hands**, while the retail longs represent fragmented **weak hands** vulnerable to margin calls [48].

#### Sentiment Metrics & Standard Thresholds

| Indicator / Service | Normal / Equilibrium | Caution / Warning Zones | Extreme Contrarian Signal Zone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Futures Bullish Consensus** *(Market Vane / Consensus)* [49, 50] | **55%** *(reflects public bullish bias)* | **> 75%** (Overbought)<br>**< 25%** (Oversold) | **> 90%** (Extreme Top)<br>**< 20%** (Extreme Bottom) |
| **Investors Intelligence** *(Advisors Poll)* [51] | Normal Range | **> 55% Bullish** (Too Optimistic)<br>**< 35% Bullish** (Too Pessimistic) | Major Top / Bottom Reversals |
| **Stocks Above 10-Wk / 30-Wk MAs** [52] | 50% | **> 70%** (Overbought)<br>**< 30%** (Oversold) | Signal triggers when crossing back inside |
| **Commitments of Traders (COT)** [53-55] | Commercial neutral | Specs Net Long / Commercials Net Short | **Fade Specs; Follow Commercials** |

#### Commitment of Traders (COT) Breakdown
* Released by the CFTC twice monthly, dividing open interest into: (1) **Commercials** (large hedgers), (2) **Large Speculators** (commodity funds), and (3) **Small Traders** (public) [53, 54].
* **Rule**: Commercial hedgers are usually right at market turns; speculators and the public are usually wrong [54].
* **Bullish Setup**: Commercials are heavily net long while Large and Small Speculators are heavily net short [54, 55].
* **Bearish Setup**: Commercials are heavily net short while Large and Small Speculators are heavily net long [55].

#### Trading & Confirmation Rules for Contrarians
1. **Wait for a Sentiment Trend Turn**: Never take a contrarian trade purely because sentiment hits an extreme (e.g., 90%) [50, 56]. **Wait until the sentiment numbers actually turn directionally** away from the extreme [50, 56].
2. **Open Interest Filter (Futures)**: Do NOT initiate a contrarian trade while open interest is still rising [57]. Wait for open interest to **flatten out or decline**, signaling that capital inflows have ceased [57].
3. **Check Hedger Participation**: Ensure commercial hedgers hold at least 50% of open interest before placing contrarian trades against speculators [58].
4. **Monitor Fundamental News Reaction**:
   * If market sentiment is overbought (>75%) and prices **fail to rally on good fundamental news**, a major top is imminent [56, 58].
   * If sentiment is oversold (<25%) and prices **fail to fall on bad fundamental news**, a bottom is established [56].
5. **Technical Confirmation**: Always require traditional chart confirmation—such as breaking a major trendline, support/resistance level, or an MACD/RSI oscillator divergence—before executing a contrarian trade [56].

#### Author Case Studies & Examples
* **Coffee Blowoff Tops**: Rallies in coffee futures were accompanied by extreme public bullishness, but open interest plummeted during the advances, signaling short covering and leading to sharp crashes [53].
* **Copper Futures COT Analysis**: A weekly copper chart reveals three major sell signals where commercial hedgers held extreme net-short positions against spec net-long holdings; in every instance, the commercial hedgers were correct [55, 59].

- "258 Chapter 10 The principle of Contrary Opinion holds that when the vast majority of people agree on anything, they are generally wrong. A true contrarian, therefore, will first try to determine what the majority are doing and then will act in the opposite direction. Humphrey B. Neill, considered the dean of contrary thinking, described his theories in a 1954 book entitled, The Art of Contrary Thinking. Ten years later, in 1964, James H. Sibbet began to apply Neill's principles to commodity futures trading by creating the Market Vane advisory service, which includes the Bullish Consensus numbers (Market Vane, P.O. The rationale behind this approach is that most futures traders are influenced to a great extent by market advisory services. By monitoring the views of the professional market letters, therefore, a reasonably accurate gauge of the attitudes of the trading public can be obtained."
- "Oscillators and Contrary Opinion 259 Contrary Opinion Measures Remaining Buying or Selling Power Consider the case of an individual speculator. Assume that speculator reads his or her favorite newsletter and becomes convinced that a market is about to move substantially higher. The more bullish the forecast, the more aggressively that trader will approach the market. Once that individual speculator's funds are fully committed to that particular market, however, he or she is overbought—meaning there are no more funds to commit to the market."
- "Expanding this situation to include all market participants, if 80-90% of market traders are bullish on a market, it is assumed that they have already taken their market positions. Who is left to buy and push the market higher? This then is one of the keys to understanding Contrary Opinion. If the overwhelming sentiment of market traders is on one side of the market, there simply isn't enough buying or selling pressure left to continue the present trend. Contrary Opinion Measures Strong Versus Weak Hands"
- "A second feature of this philosophy is its ability to compare strong versus weak hands. Futures trading is a zero sum game. For every long there is also a short. If 80% of the traders are on the long side of a market, then the remaining 20% (who are holding short positions) must be well financed enough to absorb the longs held by the other 80%. The shorts, therefore, must be holding much larger positions than the longs (in this case, 4 to 1). This means further that the shorts must be well capitalized and are considered to be strong hands. The 80%, who are holding much smaller positions per trader, are considered to be weaker hands who will be forced to liquidate those longs on any sudden turn in prices."
