### 1. Basic Candlesticks (Real Body and Shadows)

* **Core Concepts & Definitions**: Candlestick charts display the same four fundamental price points as standard Western bar charts: **Open**, **High**, **Low**, and **Close** [3, 4]. 
  * **Real Body**: The rectangular portion measuring the price distance between the Open and Close [4, 5].
    * **White Body (Open/Unfilled)**: Formed when the Close price is higher than the Open price (\\(\text{Close} > \text{Open}\\)), signaling positive/bullish price movement [4, 5].
    * **Black Body (Filled)**: Formed when the Close price is lower than the Open price (\\(\text{Close} < \text{Open}\\)), signaling negative/bearish price movement [4, 5].
  * **Shadows (Wicks / Hairs)**: Thin vertical lines extending above and below the real body representing the extreme intraday High and Low prices [4, 5].
  * **Long Days vs. Short Days**: **Long Days** represent large differences between Open and Close (large real body) [6]. **Short Days** represent small differences between Open and Close (small real body) [6].
  * **Spinning Tops**: Candlesticks with small real bodies and upper/lower shadows longer than the body [6, 7]. Body color is secondary; they represent market **indecision** [7].

* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * \\(\text{Real Body Range} = |\text{Close} - \text{Open}|\\) [5]
  * \\(\text{Total Range} = \text{High} - \text{Low}\\) [4, 5]
  * \\(\text{Upper Shadow} = \text{High} - \max(\text{Open}, \text{Close})\\) [5]
  * \\(\text{Lower Shadow} = \min(\text{Open}, \text{Close}) - \text{Low}\\) [5]

---

### 2. Doji

* **Definition & Core Concepts**: Formed when the **Open and Close prices are equal** (or nearly equal, especially in high-volatility/large-price movements) [7]. Doji lines represent extreme indecision among market participants [7, 8].
* **Doji Variations**:
  * **Long-Legged Doji**: Features long upper and lower shadows, reflecting significant intraday expansion and indecision [8].
  * **Gravestone Doji**: Has a long upper shadow and no lower shadow (\\(\text{Open} = \text{Close} = \text{Low}\\)) [8]. The longer the upper shadow, the more **bearish** the interpretation [8].
  * **Dragonfly Doji**: Opposite of the Gravestone Doji; has a long lower shadow and no upper shadow (\\(\text{Open} = \text{Close} = \text{High}\\)) [8]. Considered quite **bullish** [8].
* **Mathematical Formula**:
  * \\(\text{Close} = \text{Open} \quad \text{or} \quad |\text{Close} - \text{Open}| \le \epsilon\\) (where \\(\epsilon\\) is a minimal pixel/price threshold) [7, 9]

---

### 3. Hammer

* **Definition & Pattern**: A single-candlestick bullish reversal pattern featuring a small real body (white or black) located at the upper end of the price range and a **long lower shadow** (typically at least twice the height of the real body), with little to no upper shadow [10, 11].
* **Preceding Trend & Context**: Must occur following an established **downtrend** [10, 12].
* **Trading & Confirmation Rules**:
  * **Psychology**: Intraday sellers pressed prices significantly lower, but strong buying emerged to drive prices back toward the high, indicating potential trend exhaustion [12, 13].
  * **Confirmation**: Requires a preceding downtrend (e.g., verified via a short-term 10-period moving average) and subsequent bullish confirmation or oversold oscillator status [12, 14, 15].

---

### 4. Hanging Man

* **Definition & Pattern**: Visually identical in structure to the Hammer (small real body near the top of the range, long lower shadow), but appears at the end of an advance [11, 16].
* **Preceding Trend & Context**: Must occur during or after an **uptrend** (bearish reversal pattern) [12, 16].
* **Trading & Confirmation Rules**:
  * **Psychology**: Demonstrates that selling pressure emerged during the day [12, 13]. Even if prices recovered to close near the high, the appearance of intraday selling flags top-side vulnerability [12, 16].
  * **Confirmation**: Must be confirmed by subsequent bearish action or indicator filtering; a Hanging Man structure appearing in a downtrend is not valid [12, 15].

---

### 5. Bullish & Bearish Engulfing Patterns

* **Definition & Pattern**: Two-candle reversal formations [10, 16].
  * **Bullish Engulfing**: A two-day pattern in a downtrend. Day 1 is a small black candlestick; Day 2 is a long white candlestick whose real body completely covers/engulfs the real body of Day 1 [10, 11].
  * **Bearish Engulfing**: A two-day pattern in an uptrend. Day 1 is a small white candlestick; Day 2 is a long black candlestick whose real body completely engulfs the real body of Day 1 [11, 16].
* **Trading & Confirmation Rules**:
  * The second day's real body must completely overlap the prior day's real body [10, 11, 16].
  * Signals a sudden shift in control from bears to bulls (or vice versa) [12, 13].

---

### 6. Dark Cloud Cover

* **Definition & Pattern**: A two-day bearish reversal pattern occurring exclusively in an **uptrend** [16, 17].
  * **Day 1**: Long white candlestick reinforcing the uptrend [17].
  * **Day 2**: Opens above the high price of Day 1 (adding to initial bullish momentum), but trades lower all day to close **at or below the 50% midpoint** of Day 1's white real body (forming a black candlestick) [17, 18].
* **Mathematical Formula**:
  * \\(\text{Open}_2 > \text{High}_1\\) [17]
  * \\(\text{Close}_2 \le \text{Open}_1 + 0.5 \times (\text{Close}_1 - \text{Open}_1)\\) [17, 18]
* **Trading Rules**: Deals a blow to bullish psychology and forces long liquidation [18].

---

### 7. Piercing Line

* **Definition & Pattern**: The bullish counterpart to Dark Cloud Cover; a two-day bullish reversal pattern occurring in a **downtrend** [10, 17, 18].
  * **Day 1**: Long black candlestick solidifying downtrend confidence [19].
  * **Day 2**: Opens at a new low (below Day 1's low/close), trades higher throughout the session, and closes **above the 50% midpoint** of Day 1's black body (forming a white candlestick) [19].
* **Mathematical Formula**:
  * \\(\text{Open}_2 < \text{Low}_1\\) [19]
  * \\(\text{Close}_2 \ge \text{Open}_1 - 0.5 \times (\text{Open}_1 - \text{Close}_1)\\) [19]
* **Trading Rules**: Forces short covering and invites new buying interest [19].

---

### 8. Morning Star and Evening Star

* **Evening Star (Bearish Reversal)**: A three-day pattern occurring in an **uptrend** [19-21].
  * **Day 1**: Long white candlestick [20, 21].
  * **Day 2 (Star)**: Small real body gapping above Day 1's real body [21].
  * **Day 3**: Opens with a gap below the Star's body and closes lower, with Close price penetrating below the midpoint of Day 1's white body [21].
* **Morning Star (Bullish Reversal)**: A three-day pattern occurring in a **downtrend** [19, 20].
  * **Day 1**: Long black candlestick [19, 20].
  * **Day 2 (Star)**: Small real body gapping below Day 1's body [11, 20].
  * **Day 3**: Long white candlestick closing well into Day 1's black body [11, 20].
* **Subjective vs. Computer Rules**: Automated/computer pattern recognition mandates strict gap and midpoint criteria, whereas visual chartists allow minor flexibility (e.g., slight overlap without explicit gaps) [21, 22].

---

### 9. Filtered Candle Patterns Combined with Western Indicators

* **Core Concept**: Introduced by Greg Morris in 1991, **candle pattern filtering** combines traditional Western oscillators with Japanese candlestick patterns to eliminate false signals and improve overall timing [15].
* **The "Presignal Area" Rule**:
  * Reversal candle patterns are evaluated **only when a Western oscillator is in its presignal extreme area** [15, 23].
  * **Stochastics %D Example**: The presignal buy zone is \\(\%D < 20\\) (oversold), and the presignal sell zone is \\(\%D > 80\\) (overbought) [23, 24].
  * If a reversal pattern appears while the oscillator is at a neutral level (e.g., \\(\%D = 65\\)), the pattern is **ignored** [23].
  * Applicable oscillators include **Stochastics %D, Welles Wilder's RSI, Donald Lambert's CCI, and Larry Williams' %R** [24, 25].
  * Applies **strictly to reversal candle patterns**, not continuation patterns [23].

* **Author Case Study (3Com Corp)**:
  * **Setup**: In a chart of 3Com Corp (`COMS`), a **Piercing Line** bullish reversal pattern developed at a market trough [24].
  * **Filter Verification**: Simultaneously, the **Stochastic %D** indicator was positioned in its presignal buy zone (below 20) [24].
  * **Result**: The presignal alignment validated the Piercing Line pattern, generating a high-probability buy signal before traditional moving average or oscillator crossover signals occurred [24, 25].
