### 1. Definition of Trend

* **General Definition**: In a broad sense, a trend is simply the direction of the market—the direction in which prices are moving [1, 2].
* **Technical Definition**: Prices do not move in a straight line; rather, market action is characterized by a series of zigzags resembling waves with distinct peaks and troughs [2]. The direction of these **peaks and troughs** constitutes the market trend [2].
* **Dow Theory Foundation**: The concept originates from Dow Theory, which defines an uptrend as a pattern of rising peaks and troughs (successive rally highs closing higher than previous peaks and successive rally lows closing higher than previous troughs) and a downtrend as successively lower peaks and troughs [3].

---

### 2. Three Directions of Trend

Markets move in **three distinct directions**: **up**, **down**, and **sideways** (trendless) [4].

* **Uptrend**: Characterized by a series of **successively higher peaks and troughs** [3, 5].
* **Downtrend**: Characterized by a series of **declining (successively lower) peaks and troughs** [3, 5].
* **Sideways / Trendless Trend**: Characterized by **horizontal peaks and troughs** [4, 5]. This reflects a period of price equilibrium where the forces of supply and demand are in relative balance (referred to in Dow Theory as a "line") [4].
* **Market Dynamics & Duration**: Prices spend at least **one-third of the time in sideways trading ranges** [4]. Most technical tools and trading systems are trend-following in nature and fail or produce heavy losses during horizontal, trendless periods [6].
* **Three Strategic Choices**: At any time, a trader has three choices: buy (go long), sell (go short), or stand aside (do nothing) [7]. In a sideways market, staying out of the market is usually the wisest choice [7].

---

### 3. Three Classifications (Degrees) of Trend

Trends exist across interacting time dimensions and are categorized into **three main classifications**:

| Trend Classification | Dow Theory Duration (Stocks) | Murphy Adaptation (Futures) | Ocean Analogy (Dow) | Primary Trading Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Major (Primary)** | > 1 Year [8, 9] | > 6 Months [9] | **Tide** [8] | Overall market direction [8, 10] |
| **Intermediate (Secondary)** | 3 Weeks to 3 Months [9, 11] | 3 Weeks to 3 Months [9] | **Waves** [8] | Primary trend-following trading vehicle; corrections retrace \\(33\%\text{--}67\%\\) (often \\(50\%\\)) [11, 12] |
| **Near-Term (Minor)** | < 3 Weeks [9, 10] | < 2–3 Weeks [9] | **Ripples** [8] | Entry and exit timing [12, 13] |

* **Interrelationship of Degrees**: Each trend is part of the next larger trend and is comprised of smaller trends [14]. For instance, an intermediate correction (labeled \\(2\text{--}3\\)) in a major uptrend consists of minor waves (\\(A\text{--}B\text{--}C\\)) [14-16].

---

### 4. Support and Resistance Psychology

* **Definitions**:
  * **Support**: A price level or area under the market where buying interest is strong enough to overcome selling pressure, halting a decline and turning prices upward [17]. Identified by a previous reaction low [17].
  * **Resistance**: A price level or area above the market where selling pressure overcomes buying pressure, halting an advance and turning prices downward [17, 18]. Identified by a previous rally peak [17, 18].
* **Market Participant Psychology**:
  * **The Longs**: Bought at the support area; delighted as prices rise, regret not buying more, and pledge to add to positions if prices dip back to support [19].
  * **The Shorts**: Sold short at support; realize they are in losing positions and pray for a price dip back to their break-even entry point to exit without a loss [19].
  * **The Uncommitted**: Undecided or liquidated positions; recognize market strength and wait for the next pullback to go long [19].
  * **Result**: All three groups create concentrated buy orders at the previous support area on pullbacks [19, 20].
* **Determinants of Significance**:
  1. **Time Spent**: The longer prices fluctuate in a support/resistance area, the more significant it becomes [20].
  2. **Volume**: Higher trading volume in an area increases its importance because more participants hold a vested interest [20].
  3. **Recency**: The more recent the trading activity, the stronger its psychological impact on market participants [21].

---

### 5. Role Reversal & Confirmation Rules

* **Role Reversal Principle**: Whenever a support or resistance level is penetrated by a significant margin, they reverse roles [22-24]:
  * **Penetrated Support becomes Resistance**: Former buy orders beneath the market turn into sell orders above the market due to trapped longs liquidating on bounces and shorts adding positions [21, 25, 26].
  * **Penetrated Resistance becomes Support**: Former sell resistance turns into buying support on subsequent market pullbacks [24, 26].
  * **Trendline Reversal**: Broken up trendlines (support) become resistance; broken down trendlines (resistance) become support [27].

#### Penetration & Confirmation Rules (Mathematical Filters)
To confirm whether a penetration is valid or merely a intraday spike, technicians apply specific filters:

1. **Percentage Penetration Filter**:
   \\[\text{Price Penetration \%} = \left| \frac{\text{Closing Price} - \text{Support/Resistance Level}}{\text{Support/Resistance Level}} \right| \times 100\%\\]
   * **3% Rule**: Standard benchmark filter for major support/resistance levels [28].
   * **1% Rule**: Used for short-term support/resistance levels [28].
2. **Time Filter (2-Day Rule)**: Requires two consecutive closes beyond the support or resistance level to confirm a valid breakout [29].

---

### 6. Round Numbers as Support and Resistance

* **Psychological Concept**: Markets tend to stop advances or declines at major round numbers (e.g., \\(10\\), \\(20\\), \\(25\\), \\(50\\), \\(75\\), \\(100\\), and multiples of \\(1{,}000\\)) because traders naturally think in round figures as target objectives [30].

#### Trading & Order Rules for Round Numbers
* **Entry Orders**:
  * **Buy Limit Orders**: Place orders **just above** an important round number (e.g., \\(99.10\\) or \\(101.00\\) instead of \\(100.00\\)) because heavy buying interest at the round figure may prevent the market from reaching it [31].
  * **Sell Limit Orders**: Place resting sell limit orders **just below** key round numbers [31].
* **Protective Stop Orders**:
  * **Long Positions**: Place protective sell stops **below** key round numbers [31, 32].
  * **Short Positions**: Place protective buy stops **above** key round numbers [31, 32].

### 7. Author Case Studies

1. **Gold Market Case Study (Round Numbers)**:
   * The \\(1982\\) bear market low in gold stopped right at **\$300** [30].
   * The initial rally in \\(1983\\) peaked just above **\$500** before declining to **\$400** [30].
   * The \\(1987\\) gold rally stalled again at **\$500** [30].
   * From \\(1990\\) to \\(1997\\), gold failed every attempt to break through the **\$400** level [30].
2. **Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA)**:
   * Demonstrates a recurring historical tendency to stall or encounter major resistance at multiples of **1,000** [30].
3. **Nasdaq 100 Index Case Study (Role Reversal)**:
   * Early \\(1997\\) resistance peak on the Nasdaq 100 was broken to the upside; a year later (early \\(1998\\)), an intermediate market decline found support right at that exact prior resistance peak [33].
