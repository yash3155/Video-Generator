### Complex and Failed Head and Shoulders

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Complex Head and Shoulders**: A variation of the classic Head and Shoulders reversal pattern where multiple peaks or troughs appear, such as a double left shoulder, a double right shoulder, or a double head [1]. These formations maintain a strong tendency toward **symmetry**—a single left shoulder usually presages a single right shoulder, whereas a double left shoulder significantly increases the probability of a double right shoulder [1].
* **Failed Head and Shoulders**: A pattern that initially develops like a standard Head and Shoulders reversal, but at some point (either prior to or shortly after breaking the neckline), prices reverse direction, recross the neckline, and resume their original trend [2, 3].

#### Confirmation Rules & Volume Dynamics
* **Neckline Completion**: A Head and Shoulders pattern is not complete until prices decisively break the neckline on a closing basis [4, 5]. 
* **Failure Signal**: Once the neckline has been broken, prices must not recross it [2, 3]. A decisive close back above a top neckline (or below a bottom neckline) signals that the initial breakdown was a bad signal, confirming pattern failure [3].
* **Volume Profile**: The head forms on lighter volume than the left shoulder [6, 7]. The right shoulder displays noticeably lighter trading activity [6, 7]. The breakout across the neckline should show expanding volume [4, 7], while any subsequent return move back to the neckline occurs on light volume [6, 8]. On inverse (bottom) Head and Shoulders patterns, a sharp burst of heavy volume on the neckline breakout is mandatory for completion [9, 10].

#### Trading Rules & Tactics
* **Early Entry Tactics (Bottoms)**: Technical traders often initiate initial long positions while the right shoulder is still forming [2]. Common entry triggers include a 1/2 to 2/3 pullback of the rally from the head, a decline to the price level of the left shoulder, or the breaking of a short-term down trendline [2].
* **Confirmation Entry**: Additional position units can be added upon the decisive closing penetration of the neckline or on the return move back to the neckline [2, 8].
* **Defensive Action**: If prices recross the neckline after a breakout (confirming a failed pattern), traders must exit losing positions immediately to protect capital [3].

#### Mathematical Formulas & Measuring Techniques
The price target is derived from the vertical height of the pattern projected from the breakout point [11, 12]:

\\[\text{Height } (H) = |\text{Price}_{\text{Head}} - \text{Price}_{\text{Neckline at Head}}|\\] [12]

\\[\text{Price Objective (Top)} = \text{Breakout Price}_{\text{Neckline}} - H\\] [12]

\\[\text{Price Objective (Bottom)} = \text{Breakout Price}_{\text{Neckline}} + H\\] [9, 12]

* **Variables**:
  * \\(\text{Price}_{\text{Head}}\\): Extreme price reached at the apex of the head [12].
  * \\(\text{Price}_{\text{Neckline at Head}}\\): Price level along the neckline directly vertical to the head apex [12].
  * \\(H\\): Vertical distance (height/volatility) of the pattern [12, 13].
  * \\(\text{Breakout Price}_{\text{Neckline}}\\): Exact price point where the neckline is penetrated on a closing basis [12].

* **Alternative Wave Projection Formula**:
  \\[\text{Price Objective} = \text{Breakout Price} \pm (2 \times |\text{Price}_{\text{Head}} - \text{Price}_{\text{Trough D}}|)\\] [14]
  * Where \\(\text{Price}_{\text{Trough D}}\\) is the reaction trough following the head [14, 15].
  * *Note*: This target represents a **minimum price objective** [12, 13]. The maximum potential objective is a complete 100% retracement of the prior trend [13].

### Triple Tops and Triple Bottoms

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* A major trend reversal pattern consisting of **three prominent peaks or troughs** occurring at approximately the same price level [23]. 
* It is a minor structural variation of the Head and Shoulders pattern (where the middle peak/trough is equal in height to the outer two rather than higher or lower) [23]. Both patterns share identical forecasting implications [23].

#### Confirmation Rules & Volume Dynamics
* **Triple Top Confirmation**: The pattern is complete only when support levels along **both intervening reaction lows** have been broken on a closing basis [24, 25].
* **Triple Bottom Confirmation**: Complete only when prices close through **both intervening rally peaks** [24, 25]. (An alternate strategy utilizes the breaking of the nearest peak or trough as an early reversal signal) [24].
* **Volume Pattern**: Volume tends to diminish with each successive peak or trough during formation [24, 25]. Heavy downside volume is expected at the breakdown point of a top [24, 25], while a sharp burst of upside volume is mandatory to confirm a bottom [24, 25].

#### Trading Rules
* Await a decisive closing breakout beyond both intervening reaction points before establishing major reversal positions [24, 25].
* A return move back to the broken support or resistance line following the breakout is common and provides a secondary entry opportunity [25, 26].

#### Mathematical Formulas & Measuring Techniques
\\[\text{Pattern Height } (H) = \text{Price}_{\text{Peaks}} - \text{Price}_{\text{Lowest Trough}}\\] [25]

\\[\text{Downside Target} = \text{Breakdown Price} - H\\] [25]

\\[\text{Upside Target} = \text{Breakout Price} + H\\] [25, 26]

* **Variables**:
  * \\(\text{Price}_{\text{Peaks}}\\): Horizontal price level aligned with the three peaks [25].
  * \\(\text{Price}_{\text{Lowest Trough}}\\): Price level of the lowest reaction low formed between the peaks [25].
  * \\(H\\): Vertical height of the formation [25, 26].

#### Author Case Studies
* **Kaufman & Broad Home (Weekly Chart)**: Formed a major two-year Triple Bottom (1995–1997) holding strong support just below the 12 level three separate times before launching a major bull advance [26].

### Double Tops (M) and Double Bottoms (W)

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* The second most common and easily recognized reversal pattern after the Head and Shoulders [27].
* **Double Top ("M")**: Formed by two prominent peaks at approximately the same price level during an uptrend [27, 28].
* **Double Bottom ("W")**: Formed by two prominent troughs at approximately the same price level during a downtrend [27-29].

#### Confirmation Rules & Volume Dynamics
* **Completion Requirement**: A Double Top is merely **potential** until the price actually violates the middle reaction low (point B) on a closing basis [28, 30, 31]. A Double Bottom is confirmed only upon a decisive close above the middle reaction peak [28, 29].
* **Time Separation**: Valid double tops or bottoms generally require at least **one month** between the two peaks or troughs (and can span two to three months or several years) [32].
* **Volume Dynamics**: Trading volume is usually heavier on the first peak/trough and lighter on the second [27, 31]. Volume expands noticeably on the decisive breakout through the middle reaction point [27, 31].

#### Trading Rules & Risk Management
* Avoid taking premature positions on the second peak/trough while prices remain within the range [28, 33].
* Wait for a decisive close beyond the middle trough/peak to confirm trend reversal [28, 31]. Return moves back to the breakout level offer lower-risk entry points [27, 31].

#### Mathematical Formulas & Measuring Techniques
* **Standard Vertical Projection**:
  \\[\text{Height } (H) = |\text{Price}_{\text{Peak}} - \text{Price}_{\text{Middle Trough}}|\\] [31]
  \\[\text{Downside Target} = \text{Breakdown Price} - H\\] [31]
  \\[\text{Upside Target} = \text{Breakout Price} + H\\] [29, 34]

* **Alternate Downleg/Upleg Method**:
  \\[\text{Price Target} = \text{Price}_{\text{Middle Trough}} - (\text{Price}_{\text{Peak A}} - \text{Price}_{\text{Trough B}})\\] [34]
  * Where \\((\text{Price}_{\text{Peak A}} - \text{Price}_{\text{Trough B}})\\) is the vertical length of the initial downleg [34].

#### Author Case Studies
* **Applied Materials Inc. (AMAT, Daily Chart)**: Formed a two-month Double Top near 52 (where the second peak fell slightly short of the first), giving a sell signal when support near 46 was broken [30].
* **United Technologies Corp. (UTX, Daily Chart)**: Formed a Double Bottom by bouncing off 68 twice across three months (with the second trough acting as an upside reversal day), completing the pattern upon breaking resistance at 80 [29].
* **Nasdaq Composite Index (Daily Chart)**: Formed a Double Bottom near 1470, confirmed by the breaking of an overhead down trendline [35].

### Bull Traps / False Breakouts & Filters

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Bull Trap / False Breakout**: Occurs near the end of an advance when price briefly penetrates above a previous peak or resistance level, giving the appearance that the uptrend has resumed, but quickly reverses downward to complete a topping pattern [36-38].

#### Volume & Filter Rules
* **Volume Clue**: A false breakout is typically characterized by **light volume on the upside breakout** followed immediately by **heavy volume on the subsequent decline**—a signature negative combination [37, 38].
* **Price Filter (1%–3% Rule)**: Requires a closing penetration beyond the previous peak by a minimum threshold of 1% to 3% [36, 39].
* **Time Filter (2-Day Rule)**: Requires prices to close beyond the breakout level for **two consecutive days** [36, 40].
* **Weekly Closing Filter**: Requires a Friday close beyond the breakout point to confirm a valid signal [36, 40].

#### Trading Rules
* Apply time and price filters to breakout signals to filter out whipsaws [36, 37, 40].
* If price breaks out above a peak on light volume and subsequently closes back below resistance on heavy volume, exit long positions immediately [37, 38].

#### Author Case Studies
* **J.P. Morgan & Co. (JPM, Daily Chart)**: Displayed a classic bull trap where price briefly exceeded the 122 peak on light volume before collapsing back down on heavy volume [38].

### Normal Pullbacks vs. Double Tops

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Structural Distinction**: The term "double top" is greatly overused in financial markets [41]. Prices naturally tend to back off from a previous peak or bounce off a previous low; these temporary pullbacks are normal trend behavior, not automatic reversal patterns [33, 41, 42].
* **Normal Pullback**: A temporary corrective dip in an ongoing uptrend that backs off from a prior high but holds above support [33, 42].

#### Distinguishing Rules & Guidelines
* **Technical Odds Principle**: Technical analysis assumes that the existing trend will continue until a reversal is fully confirmed [33].
* **Reversal Condition**: A Double Top **only exists** once support at the middle reaction low (point B) is broken on a closing basis [41, 42]. 
* If prices back off from a peak but hold above point B, the move is treated as a normal pullback preceding a trend resumption [33, 42].

#### Trading Rules
* Do not initiate reversal trades prematurely during an initial pullback from a peak [33]. Await a confirmed break of support before altering trend assumptions [33, 42].

### Saucers (Rounding Tops and Bottoms)

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **Saucer / Rounding Bottom**: A major reversal pattern showing an extremely slow, gradual transition in market trend from down to sideways to up [43, 44].
* **Rounding Top**: An inverted saucer representing a slow, circular rounding off at a market top [45, 46].

#### Characteristics & Volume Profile
* **Time Horizon**: Typically identified on long-term weekly or monthly bar charts spanning several years [43].
* **Volume Behavior**: Volume gradually mirrors price action—contracting to a minimum as the market bottoms out sideways, and slowly expanding as the new uptrend gains traction [45].
* **Targeting Limit**: It is difficult to pinpoint the exact completion point or calculate precise numerical price targets due to the absence of a sharp breakout boundary [43, 47]. The longer the saucer lasts, the greater the subsequent move [43].

#### Author Case Studies
* **Bristol Myers Squibb (Monthly Chart)**: Exhibited a major 4-year Saucer Bottom (1992–1996) that gradually rounded out before launching a powerful multi-year advance [48].

### V-Spike Reversals

#### Core Concepts & Definitions
* **V-Spike (Spike Reversal)**: An abrupt market reversal that occurs rapidly with little to no transition period or chart consolidation [49].
* It typically occurs in a market that has become severely overextended in one direction, where a sudden piece of adverse news or extreme sentiment exhaustion triggers an immediate turnaround [49, 50].

#### Identification & Technical Characteristics
* **Telltale Warning**: Often the only advance warning given is a single daily or weekly **Key Reversal Day** (or Outside Day) occurring on **unusually heavy volume** [49, 51-53].
* **Cycle Dynamics**: Under cycle analysis, a V-reversal occurs when an intermediate price cycle turns simultaneously with its next longer and next shorter duration cycle components [54].

#### Trading Rules & Risk Management
* V-spikes are the most difficult market turns to predict or trade using classic visual chart patterns [49].
* **Risk Control**: Because pattern setup time is absent, traders must monitor technical indicators (such as percentage envelopes, moving average distance, and overbought/oversold oscillators) to identify overextended market conditions before a spike occurs [49, 55, 56].

#### Author Case Studies
* **Micron Technology Inc. (MU, Daily Chart)**: Showed a violent V-Reversal top where a rapid rally to near 60 reversed abruptly into a steep drop on heavy volume without forming a topping pattern [53].
* **Dow Jones Industrial Average (Daily Chart, Oct 28, 1997)**: Formed a classic upside V-reversal / selling climax bottom where prices opened sharply lower, reversed intraday on record volume, and closed near the daily highs [57].

- "What Constitutes a Valid Breaking of a Trendline? As a general rule, a close beyond the trendline is more significant than just an intraday penetration. To go a step further, sometimes even a closing penetration is not enough. Most technicians employ a variety of time and price filters in an attempt to isolate valid trendline penetrations and eliminate bad signals or "whipsaws." One example of a price filter is the 3% penetration criteria. This price filter is used mainly for the breaking of longer term trend-lines, but requires that the trendline be broken, on a closing basis, by at least 3%. (The 3% rule doesn't apply to some financial futures, such as the interest rate markets.)"
- "An alternative to a price filter (requiring that a trendline be broken by some predetermined price increment or percentage amount) is a time filter. A common time filter is the two day rule. In other words, to have a valid breaking of a trendline, prices must close beyond the trendline for two successive days. To break 72 Chapter 4 an up trendline, therefore, prices must close under the trendline two days in a row. A one day violation would not count. The 1-3% rule and the two day rule are also applied to the breaking of important support and resistance levels, not just to major trend-lines. Another filter would require a Friday close beyond a major breakout point to ensure a weekly signal."
- "The Term "Double Top" Greatly Overused The terms "double top and bottom" are greatly overused in the financial markets. Most potential double tops or bottoms wind up being something else. These price changes are a natural reaction and do not in themselves constitute a reversal pattern. Remember that, at a top, prices must actually violate the previous reaction low before the double top exists."

BLOWOFFS AND SELLING CLIMAXES One final situation not covered so far that deserves mention is the type of dramatic market action that often takes place at tops and bottoms—blowoffs and selling climaxes. Blowoffs occur at major market tops and selling climaxes at bottoms. In futures, blowoffs are often accompanied by a drop in open interest during the final rally. In the case of a blowoff at market tops, prices suddenly begin to rally sharply after a long advance, accompanied by a large jump in trading activity and then peak abruptly. (See Figure 7.12.) In a selling climax bottom, prices suddenly drop sharply on heavy trading activity and rebound as quickly. (Refer back to Figure 4.22c.)"
- "A reversal day takes place either at a top or a bottom. The generally accepted definition of a top reversal day is the setting of a new high in an uptrend, followed by a lower close on the same day. In other words, prices set a new high for a given upmove at some point during the day (usually at or near the opening) then weaken and actually close lower than the previous day's closing. A bottom reversal day would be a new low during the day followed by a higher close. The wider the range for the day and the heavier the volume, the more significant is the signal for a possible near term"
