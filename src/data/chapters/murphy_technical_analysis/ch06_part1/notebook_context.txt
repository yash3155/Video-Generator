### 1. Symmetrical Triangle (The Coil)

* **Core Concept & Definition**: The **symmetrical triangle** (also called a **coil**) is a consolidation pattern bounded by two converging trendlines: a descending upper trendline and an ascending lower trendline [1-3]. It represents a temporary pause or state of equilibrium in the prevailing trend before the original trend resumes [2, 3]. It is inherently **neutral** in form, but because it functions primarily as a continuation pattern, the forecasting expectation favors a breakout in the direction of the prior trend [2, 4].
* **Pattern Structure & Reversal Points**:
  * **Minimum Requirement**: At least **4 reversal points** (two reaction highs to draw the upper descending trendline, and two reaction lows to draw the lower ascending trendline) [2, 5].
  * **Five-Wave Wave Structure**: Triangles frequently display **6 reversal points** (three peaks and three troughs), which create 5 internal corrective waves prior to the breakout [6, 7].
* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * **Base Height (\\(H\\))**:
    \\[H = P_{\text{peak, base}} - P_{\text{trough, base}}\\]
    *Variables*: \\(P_{\text{peak, base}}\\) is the price at the highest initial peak (Point 1); \\(P_{\text{trough, base}}\\) is the lowest initial trough (Point 2) at the baseline on the far left [1, 2].
  * **Vertical Price Objective (\\(P_{\text{target}}\\))**:
    \\[P_{\text{target}} = P_{\text{breakout}} + H \quad (\text{Bullish Breakout})\\]
    \\[P_{\text{target}} = P_{\text{breakout}} - H \quad (\text{Bearish Breakdown})\\]
    *Variables*: \\(P_{\text{breakout}}\\) is the exact price level where the trendline penetration occurs (Point \\(C\\)) [8, 9].
  * **Parallel Trendline (Channel) Target Method**:
    A parallel line is drawn from the top of the baseline (Point \\(A\\)) parallel to the lower trendline [8, 9]. The upside price objective is the upper channel line, which prices often meet near the time target marked by the intersection of the converging trendlines at the apex [8, 9].
* **Confirmation Rules**:
  * **Closing Penetration**: Requires a decisive close outside either trendline; intraday penetrations do not constitute valid signals [10].
  * **Volume Expansion**: A noticeable surge in volume is mandatory on upside breakouts [11, 12].
* **Trading Rules**:
  * Align positions with the prior major trend [2].
  * Enter upon a confirmed closing price penetration of the upper or lower trendline [10].
  * Broken trendlines convert to support (in uptrends) or resistance (in downtrends) on return moves, and the **apex** serves as a vital future support/resistance level [10].
* **Author Case Studies**:
  * **Dell Computer Corp (Q4 1997)**: Formed an 18-week wide bullish symmetrical triangle on its daily chart; prices broke out on the 13th week (just beyond the \\(\frac{2}{3}\\) point) [11].
  * **Southwest Airlines (Monthly Chart)**: Formed a \\(3\frac{1}{2}\\)-year bullish symmetrical triangle on a long-term monthly chart [13].

### 2. Ascending Triangle

* **Core Concept & Definition**: An **ascending triangle** is bounded by a flat (horizontal) upper resistance trendline and a rising lower support trendline [1, 9, 14]. This structure demonstrates that buyers are more aggressive than sellers, as they are willing to buy at higher reaction lows while sellers remain stationary at a fixed overhead level [9, 14].
* **Forecasting Implications**: It is an inherently **bullish pattern** regardless of where it appears in the overall market structure [4, 9, 15]. While typically a continuation pattern in uptrends, it occasionally forms as a **major bottoming pattern** at the end of a downtrend, where a breakout above the flat upper line completes the base [16].
* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * **Base Height (\\(H = AB\\))**:
    \\[H = P_{\text{resistance}} - P_{\text{lowest trough}}\\]
    *Variables*: \\(P_{\text{resistance}}\\) is the horizontal upper resistance level; \\(P_{\text{lowest trough}}\\) is the initial reaction low at the baseline [16, 17].
  * **Price Target (\\(P_{\text{target}}\\))**:
    \\[P_{\text{target}} = P_{\text{breakout}} + H\\]
    *Variables*: \\(P_{\text{breakout}}\\) is the price level at Point \\(C\\) where the close exceeds the flat upper line [16, 17].
* **Confirmation & Trading Rules**:
  * Requires a decisive close above the horizontal resistance line [15, 17].
  * Must be accompanied by a **sharp burst in volume** upon breakout [15, 17].
  * Any return move back to the broken upper resistance line (which now acts as support) must occur on light volume [15, 17].
* **Author Case Study**:
  * **Dow Transportation Index (Late 1997)**: Formed a bullish ascending triangle on the daily bar chart with a flat upper resistance ceiling at 3400 and a rising lower support line [15].

### 3. Descending Triangle

* **Core Concept & Definition**: A **descending triangle** is the mirror image of the ascending triangle, defined by a descending upper trendline and a flat (horizontal) lower support line [1, 18, 19]. It indicates that sellers are more aggressive than buyers, pushing rally peaks progressively lower against a stationary support level [18].
* **Forecasting Implications**: It is an inherently **bearish pattern** [4, 18]. It usually appears as a continuation pattern within downtrends, but can occasionally form at **market tops**, where a decisive breakdown under the flat support line signals a major trend reversal [18, 20].
* **Mathematical Formulas & Variables**:
  * **Base Height (\\(H = AB\\))**:
    \\[H = P_{\text{highest peak}} - P_{\text{support}}\\]
    *Variables*: \\(P_{\text{highest peak}}\\) is the highest initial peak at the baseline; \\(P_{\text{support}}\\) is the horizontal lower support level [20, 21].
  * **Price Target (\\(P_{\text{target}}\\))**:
    \\[P_{\text{target}} = P_{\text{breakdown}} - H\\]
    *Variables*: \\(P_{\text{breakdown}}\\) is the point of breakdown below the support line [20, 21].
* **Confirmation & Trading Rules**:
  * Confirmed by a decisive closing penetration below the flat lower support line [18, 21].
  * Downside volume should expand on the breakdown, though upside volume confirmation is strictly essential at bottoms, markets can "fall of their own weight" [18, 22, 23].
  * The broken lower line converts to overhead resistance during return moves [18].
* **Author Case Study**:
  * **Du Pont (Autumn 1997)**: Developed a daily bearish descending triangle with a declining upper trendline and flat bottom line; broke down in early October 1997 to resolve lower [23].

### 4. The \\(\frac{2}{3}\\) to \\(\frac{3}{4}\\) Apex Time Limit Rule

* **Core Concept**: Triangles synthesize price and time boundaries [24, 25]. The converging trendlines establish price limits, while the distance from the baseline to the apex dictates time limits [24, 25].
* **Mathematical Rule & Variables**:
  \\[t_{\text{breakout}} \in \left[\frac{2}{3}W, \, \frac{3}{4}W\right]\\]
  *Variables*:
  * \\(W\\): Horizontal width (time duration) measured from the vertical base on the far left to the apex point where the converging lines intersect [24, 25].
  * \\(t_{\text{breakout}}\\): Elapsed time from the pattern's start to the breakout point [24, 25].
  * *Example Calculation*: If a triangle width \\(W\\) is **20 weeks**, the breakout must take place between the **13th and 15th week** (\\(\frac{2}{3} \times 20 = 13.33\\); \\(\frac{3}{4} \times 20 = 15\\)) [25].
* **Trading Implications & Failures**:
  * If prices remain locked inside the pattern beyond the \\(\frac{3}{4}\\) point (\\(t_{\text{breakout}} > \frac{3}{4}W\\)), the triangle **loses its potency** [24].
  * Prices will usually drift sluggishly through the apex and beyond without directional momentum [24].

---

### 5. Volume Contraction and Breakout Expansion

* **Volume Contraction Rule**: As price fluctuations narrow within a triangle, trading activity/volume must systematically diminish [11, 23, 26]. This contraction in volume is standard across all consolidation patterns [11].
* **Internal Swings Volume Clues**:
  * **Symmetrical Triangle**: Volume exhibits a subtle tendency to be heavier during price bounces in uptrends and heavier on dips in downtrends [12].
  * **Ascending Triangle**: Volume is heavier on price bounces and lighter on price dips [23].
  * **Descending Triangle**: Volume is heavier on downmoves and lighter on price bounces [23].
* **Breakout Volume Expansion Rules**:
  * **Upside Breakouts**: Must be accompanied by a **sharp, noticeable surge in trading volume** [11, 15, 22]. If an upside breakout lacks volume expansion, the validity of the pattern is severely questioned [22].
  * **Downside Breakdowns**: An increase in trading activity is usual, but heavy downside volume is not as critical initially because markets can decline from a lack of buyers ("fall of their own weight") [18, 22, 27].
  * **Return Moves**: Should always occur on **light volume**, followed by heavy volume as the breakout trend resumes [10, 11, 15, 18].

---

### 6. Measuring Techniques Summary

1. **Vertical Base Projection Method**:
   * Measure the vertical height of the pattern at its widest point (the base) [8, 16, 20].
   * Project that height vertically from the breakout/breakdown point (\\(C\\)) [8, 17, 21].
2. **Parallel Channel Trendline Method (Symmetrical Triangles)**:
   * Draw a trendline from the top of the baseline parallel to the lower ascending trendline [8, 9].
   * The parallel channel line serves as the upside price objective, which prices often reach near the projected time location of the apex [8, 9].

### 7. Broadening (Megaphone) Formation

* **Core Concept & Definition**: An unusual and relatively rare variation of the triangle that acts as an **inverted or backwards triangle** [28]. Instead of converging, the trendlines **diverge**, creating an expanding price range [28]. It is also termed a **megaphone top** [28].
* **Chart Pattern Structure**: Displays **three successively higher peaks** (Points 1, 3, 5) and **two declining reaction troughs** (Points 2, 4) [29].
* **Psychological & Market Context**:
  * Represents a market that is **out of control** and driven by unusually high emotion [29].
  * Signals uncoordinated, intense public participation, which typically surfaces near the end of a major bull market [29, 30].
* **Forecasting Implications**: Almost exclusively a **bearish pattern** that occurs at major market tops [29, 31].
* **Volume Pattern**: Volume **expands** alongside the widening price swings (directly opposing the volume contraction seen in normal triangles) [29].
* **Confirmation & Trading Rules**:
  * **Confirmation Signal**: Completed when prices break decisively below the **second reaction trough** (Point 4) [29].
  * **Trading Rule**: Unusually difficult and dangerous pattern to trade due to widespread false breakouts and expanding volatility; traders are advised to exercise extreme caution [29].
