Chapter 12 of Anna Coulling's *A Complete Guide to Volume Price Analysis* is titled **"Volume And Price – The Next Generation"** [1]. 

**Important Clarification on Scope:** Chapter 12 does **not** cover topics such as "Algorithmic Order Flow," "High-Frequency Trading (HFT) Footprints," or "Modern Microstructure Shifts," nor does it contain mathematical formulas or real-world case studies [2]. Anna Coulling explicitly notes that she presents these emerging concepts as innovative developments in volume analysis, but has not used them in her own trading and therefore does not comment on their live validity or provide case studies [2]. *(Note: High-Frequency Trading is briefly mentioned earlier in Chapter 5, where Coulling quotes a 2012 SEC report breaking HFT down into four strategies: market making, arbitrage, structural, and directional [3, 4]).*

Below is the complete, grounded extraction of all core concepts, definitions, rules, analogies, and box schematics presented in Chapter 12 [1].

### 1. Equivolume Charts

* **Origin & Definition**: Developed by Richard Arms and first published in his 1994 book *Volume Cycles In The Stock Market* [5]. Equivolume replaces the traditional time-based X-axis with **volume**, while maintaining price on the Y-axis [5].
* **Core Philosophy**: Volume is treated as more important than time [5]. By placing volume directly onto the price chart rather than as an isolated indicator at the bottom, the time element is removed so the focus rests entirely on the volume-price relationship [5].
* **Author Analogy / Quote**: Coulling cites Richard Arms's famous quote: *"If the market wore a wristwatch, it would be divided into shares, not hours"* [5].
* **Box Mechanics & Construction**:
  * **Height**: Represents the price spread (High to Low of the session, not Open to Close) [5, 6].
  * **Width**: Represents the session volume (ultra-high, high, medium, or low) [5]. Volume is normalized across the chart by dividing actual volume by the total volume displayed [7].
  * **Color**: Dictated by the closing price relative to the previous close (painted black/blue if the close is above the previous close, and red if below) [7].
* **The Four Core Equivolume Box Types**:
  1. **Box 1 (Tall & Narrow)**: Wide price spread with low volume \\(\rightarrow\\) **Anomaly** (equivalent to a wide-spread candle on low volume) [5].
  2. **Box 2 (Short & Wide)**: Narrow price spread with high volume \\(\rightarrow\\) **Anomaly** (heavy effort yielding little result) [6].
  3. **Box 3 (Normal Height & Width)**: Average price spread with average volume \\(\rightarrow\\) **Normal / Valid** relationship [6].
  4. **Box 4 (Tall & Wide / "Power Box")**: Wide price spread with high volume \\(\rightarrow\\) **Validation** (effort and result in agreement; often marks breakouts from congestion) [6, 7].
* **Author Rules & Critique**:
  * Coulling cautions that removing time turns analysis from **3-dimensional to 2-dimensional** [8].
  * According to Wyckoff's second law (**Cause and Effect**), the duration of a consolidation phase dictates the extent of the subsequent trend [8]. Removing time eliminates one of the core pillars of Volume Price Analysis—judging the potential power of a trend based on consolidation time [8].

### 2. Candlevolume Charts

* **Definition**: A hybrid chart format combining Equivolume boxes with traditional candlestick elements [9].
* **Mechanics**: The box width varies based on volume, but it retains traditional candlestick Open, High, Low, and Close points along with upper and lower wicks [9].
* **Key Feature**: Restoration of price wicks (*"wicks are back!"*) [7, 9].
* **Author Assessment**: Coulling notes that Candlevolume overcomes Equivolume's main drawback by preserving candlestick wick price action while still visually highlighting volume density through candle width [9].

---

### 3. Delta Volume

* **Definition**: Measures the net difference between the volume traded at the **ask** (buy orders) and the volume traded at the **bid** (sell orders) within a given bar [10].
* **Calculation / Formula**: 
  \\[\text{Delta Volume} = \text{Volume at Ask} - \text{Volume at Bid}\\] [10]
* **Example**: If a software feed calculates 500 contracts sold at the bid and 200 contracts bought at the ask, the net delta is \\(-300\\) contracts [10].
* **Visual Display**: Displayed as a histogram bar above the zero line for positive net buying delta or below the zero line for negative net selling delta [10].
* **Market Application**: Ideally suited for centralized, open-exchange markets like futures and equities [11].

---

### 4. Cumulative Delta Volume

* **Definition**: A running sum that collects delta volume data across consecutive bars and adds each bar's net delta to the cumulative total of previous bars [11, 12].
* **Purpose**: Provides a continuous perspective on net accumulation or distribution of buying versus selling pressure over time [11, 12].
* **Future Evolution Rule**: Coulling highlights Delta and Cumulative Delta as emerging VPA tools, predicting their growing adoption in index futures trading (such as E-mini contracts) [12].
