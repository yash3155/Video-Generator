In Anna Coulling's *A Complete Guide to Volume Price Analysis*, price action and volume are combined to decode market sentiment and insider activity [1-3]. The core concepts, definitions, formulas, analogies, and comparison frameworks across these specific topics are detailed below.

### 1. Candlestick Anatomy for VPA

**The Seven Key Elements of a Candle**
Every candlestick consists of **seven fundamental structural elements**:
1. **The Open** [4, 5]
2. **The High** [4, 5]
3. **The Low** [4, 5]
4. **The Close** [4, 5]
5. **The Upper Wick** [5]
6. **The Lower Wick** [5]
7. **The Spread** (the distance between the open and close price) [5, 6]

While all seven elements define price action within a timeframe, **the wicks and the spread are the most revealing components** of market sentiment when validated with volume [5].

**Definitions & Market Sentiment Principles**
* **The Spread**: Measures the net sentiment of the session [6]. 
  * **Wide Spread**: Indicates **strong market sentiment** (either bullish or bearish, depending on whether the close is above or below the open) [6].
  * **Narrow Spread**: Indicates **weak market sentiment**, showing a lack of conviction from market participants [6, 7].
* **The Wicks**: Indicate a **change in sentiment** during the session [6, 8]. If sentiment remains firm and undisputed from open to close, no wicks are formed [6, 9].

**Author Analogy: The Auction**
Coulling compares a candle with no wicks to an **online or physical auction** where bidding opens at one price and closes cleanly at a higher price [6]. The absence of wicks represents pure, unresisted directional momentum [6].

### 2. Sine Wave Oscillations Inside the Spread

**Concept & Definition**
Regardless of timeframe—from a tick chart to a monthly chart—price action within a candle can be visualised as a **sine wave**, oscillating back and forth as buyers and sellers battle for control [10].

**How the Oscillation Forms the Candle Structure**
* **Bullish Wave Path**: As illustrated in the text (Fig 3.11), the price opens, moves lower to establish the session low (creating the trough of the wave and the lower wick), swings upward across the spread to hit the session high (forming the peak of the wave and the upper wick), and finally retreats slightly to close above the opening price [6, 10].
* **Intrasession Journey**: Although the market may take various pullbacks or sub-reversals inside the bar, the completed candle captures this wave-like struggle between buying and selling pressure [10, 11].

---

### 3. The Diagnostic Power of Upper vs. Lower Wicks

**Core Principle**
The length and context of any wick is **always the first point of focus** in VPA analysis because it instantly reveals impending strength, weakness, indecision, and sentiment changes [8, 12].

#### **A. Lower Wick Anatomy & Diagnosis**
* **Intrasession Process**: Price opens, and sellers immediately push the market lower, dominating the first half of the candle [13]. At lower price levels, buyers enter the market, wrestling control from sellers as the price becomes attractive [14]. Buyers flood in, absorbing all selling pressure, and drive the price back up to close near or at the opening price [14, 15].
* **Diagnostic Meaning**:
  1. **Complete Reversal of Sentiment**: Selling pressure in the first part of the session was completely absorbed and overwhelmed by buying pressure in the second half [15].
  2. **Bullish Closing Sentiment**: The market closed rising, supported by underlying buying pressure [15].
  3. **Zero-Spread Significance**: A candle with a deep lower wick and zero spread (close equals open) is just as significant as a wide-spread candle [16].

#### **B. Upper Wick Anatomy & Diagnosis**
* **Intrasession Process**: Price opens, and buyers take initial control, driving prices higher [17]. As the session progresses, higher prices meet resistance; buyers exhaust their momentum, and sellers push prices back down to close near or at the opening price [17, 18].
* **Diagnostic Meaning**:
  1. **Complete Reversal of Sentiment**: Early bullish sentiment turns into bearish dominance [18].
  2. **Bearish Closing Sentiment**: Price ends the session falling under heavy selling pressure [17, 18].

#### **Key Rules for Wick Analysis**
* **No Wick**: Signals **strong, uninterrupted market sentiment** in the direction of the closing price [6, 9].
* **Premier Wick Patterns**: 
  * **Shooting Star**: Characterised by a deep upper wick and narrow body, signalling potential **market weakness and forced selling** by insiders [19, 20].
  * **Hammer**: Characterised by a deep lower wick and narrow body, signalling potential **market strength and forced buying** by insiders [20, 21].
* **Timeframe Context**: Wick signals on daily or weekly charts have a significantly greater market effect than those on 1-minute or 5-minute charts [22].

### 4. VPA Linear Confirmation vs. VAP Volume Profile

| Feature | **VPA (Volume Price Analysis)** | **VAP (Volume at Price)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Focus** | Linear relationship between volume and price **over time** [23]. | Volume distribution across **price levels** [23-25]. |
| **Chart Display** | Horizontal volume bars along the bottom (X-axis time) [25, 26]. | Vertical volume histogram along the side (Y-axis price) [25, 26]. |
| **Analytical Scope** | Big-picture "outside" view of candle-by-candle price trends [27]. | Detailed "inside" view cutting open the candle to show volume concentrations [27, 28]. |
| **Key Output** | Validates whether price moves are genuine or anomalies/traps [29]. | Visually reveals exact density of **Support and Resistance** zones [28, 30]. |

**Linear Confirmation vs. Volume Profile Relationship**
* **Complementary Triangulation**: VAP does not replace traditional VPA; it enhances it [26, 30]. VPA monitors linear momentum over time, while VAP maps price congestion levels to confirm platforms of support or ceilings of resistance [28, 30, 31].
* **Making Invisible Barriers Visible**: Where classic technical analysis relies on subjective line drawing, VAP provides a visual volume histogram proving where buying and selling density occurred [30, 32].

#### **Real-World Case Study: Microsoft (MSFT) 15-Minute Chart**
* **Setup**: Coulling highlights a 15-minute MSFT chart over a 5-day period containing four distinct price congestion phases [26, 33].
* **VAP Analysis**:
  1. The bottom congestion phase showed extreme volume concentration on the VAP histogram, defining a **massive support platform** for a breakout higher [33, 34].
  2. At the top of the advance, a narrow price range generated an extreme VAP volume bar over just one day—equalling the volume density of a prior 3-day consolidation [32, 35].
* **Diagnostic Finding & Market Reaction**: The high VAP volume at the top without price advancing revealed **heavy insider selling** [35]. Immediately at the next market open, MSFT stock fell \$1.40 like a stone, validating the VAP resistance signal [35, 36].
