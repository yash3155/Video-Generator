### Core Concepts & Definitions

* **Support (The Floor)**: An invisible price boundary beneath the market where falling prices find a cushion that stops further decline and pushes price action higher [1].
* **Resistance (The Ceiling)**: An invisible price barrier above the market where rising prices encounter resistance that prevents further upward movement and pushes price action lower [1, 2].
* **Price Congestion (Consolidation)**: A period of sideways price action oscillating within a defined channel bounded by a floor of support and a ceiling of resistance [1, 3].
* **Isolated Pivot High**: A three-bar reversal pattern where the center candle posts both a higher high and a higher low than the candles immediately to its left and right [3]. It defines the initial **ceiling** of a congestion zone [3].
* **Isolated Pivot Low**: A three-bar reversal pattern where the center candle posts both a lower low and a lower high than the candles immediately to its left and right [3]. It defines the initial **floor** of a congestion zone [3].
* **Clear Water**: A clearly visible gap between the closing price of a breakout candle and the support/resistance line being breached, signaling a true breakout rather than a minor price spike [4].
* **Fake Out**: A false breakout past support or resistance occurring on low volume, manipulated by market insiders to trap retail traders on the wrong side of the market [5, 6].

### Structural Formulas & Technical Patterns

While Volume Price Analysis (VPA) is an **art rather than a strict mathematical science** [7-9], Anna Coulling provides specific structural criteria and ratio rules for identifying support, resistance, and breakouts:

* **The 70/30 Consolidation-to-Trend Ratio**:
  \\[\text{Market Time} \approx 70\% \text{ Sideways (Consolidation)} + 30\% \text{ Trending}\\]
  Markets spend approximately 70% of their time building cause in congestion and only 30% moving in active trends [1].
* **Isolated Pivot High Formula**:
  \\[\text{High}_{\text{Center}} > \max(\text{High}_{\text{Left}}, \text{High}_{\text{Right}}) \quad \text{AND} \quad \text{Low}_{\text{Center}} > \max(\text{Low}_{\text{Left}}, \text{Low}_{\text{Right}})\\]
  Identifies the upper boundary (ceiling) of new price congestion [3].
* **Isolated Pivot Low Formula**:
  \\[\text{Low}_{\text{Center}} < \min(\text{Low}_{\text{Left}}, \text{Low}_{\text{Right}}) \quad \text{AND} \quad \text{High}_{\text{Center}} < \min(\text{High}_{\text{Left}}, \text{High}_{\text{Right}})\\]
  Identifies the lower boundary (floor) of new price congestion [3].
* **Valid Breakout Formula**:
  \\[\text{Valid Breakout} = \text{Clear Water Close} + \text{High / Rising Volume}\\]
  A true breakout requires a decisive candle close past the barrier combined with above-average, rising volume [4, 6].
* **Valid Pullback Re-test Rule**:
  \\[\text{Valid Re-test} = \text{Price Pullback to Broken Level} + \text{Low / Falling Volume}\\]
  A pullback toward broken support or resistance must occur on low volume to confirm trend continuation [10, 11].

### Key Rules by Topic

#### 1. The 70/30 Consolidation vs. Trend Law
* **Time Distribution**: Markets move sideways roughly 70% of the time and trend only 30% of the time [1].
* **Three Causes of Sideways Movement**:
  1. **Fundamental News**: Pending economic data releases (e.g., Non-Farm Payrolls) lock price into narrow ranges while traders wait for data [1].
  2. **Insider Operations**: Buying or selling climaxes, during which specialists and market makers fill or empty their inventory (accumulation/distribution) [1].
  3. **Old Price Barriers**: Price running into historic price levels where traders remain trapped in weak positions from prior market moves [1].
* **Cause & Effect Relationship**: Based on Wyckoff's second law, the longer a market consolidates (the cause), the more dramatic and long-lasting the resulting trend will be (the effect) [12, 13].

#### 2. The House Analogy (Floors Becoming Ceilings)
* **Resistance Turns to Support**: When price rises through the ceiling of a floor level (e.g., Ground Floor), that former ceiling becomes the floor of support for the next level up (e.g., First Floor) [14, 15].
* **Support Turns to Resistance**: When price breaks down through the floor of a level, that former floor becomes the ceiling of resistance for the level below [16, 17].
* **Natural Barrier & Protection**: Broken support or resistance acts as a physical cushion [1, 18]. It protects existing trades from pullbacks and serves as a logical location to place stop-loss orders on the opposite side of congestion [18-20].
* **The "Rubber Band" Rule**: Support and resistance lines are **not rigid rods of steel**; they are flexible, elastic bands with "give" [4, 8]. A minor price tick beyond the line does not equal a breakout without a clean close and volume confirmation [4, 8].

#### 3. Trapped Emotional Buyers & Sellers
* **Weak Traders at Wave Tops**:
  * During a rally inside a congestion channel, retail traders buy near the top out of greed [21, 22]. 
  * When price immediately reverses, these buyers are trapped in a losing position, experiencing rising fear [21, 23].
  * As price returns to the top on a subsequent wave, fear turns to relief, prompting trapped buyers to sell out at breakeven or with a small loss [21, 23, 24]. This collective selling creates the resistance ceiling [21, 24].
* **Strong Traders at Wave Bottoms**:
  * Traders who buy on pullbacks at the floor are patient and experienced ("strong traders") [25, 26].
  * They manage potential profits rather than stress over unrealized losses, providing solid support at the channel floor [23, 25].
* **Psychological Mechanics**: Support and resistance zones represent dense populations of trapped buyers and sellers whose fear and greed turn historical price levels into self-fulfilling price barriers [22, 26, 27].

### Author Analogies

* **The House Cross-Section**: Visualizing price action moving through a multi-story house (ground floor, first floor, second floor, roof) to explain how ceilings become floors and floors become ceilings [14-16, 28].
* **Ping-Pong / Arcade Game**: Oscillations of price bouncing endlessly back and forth between the floor of support and ceiling of resistance [2].
* **Rubber Bands vs. Rods of Steel**: Lines drawn on charts are flexible elastic bands that can stretch, not rigid steel bars [4, 8].
* **Quicksand / Bog**: Breaking away from a congested price zone requires massive effort and rising volume, like pulling a heavy object out of quicksand [6].
* **Spawning Grounds**: Price congestion areas act as the natural spawning grounds where new trends are born [29, 30].
* **Sailing GPS vs. Paper Map**: Pivot points act as a quick GPS plotter on a boat, while Volume Price Analysis provides the underlying paper chart and compass [31].
