### 1. The Five Core Principles of Candlestick VPA
Before evaluating specific candles, Coulling establishes five foundational principles that govern all Volume Price Analysis [4]:

1. **Wick Length as Primary Focus:** The length of any wick (upper or lower) is **ALWAYS** the first point of focus because it instantly reveals impending strength, weakness, indecision, and market sentiment [4].
2. **Absence of Wicks:** If no wick is created, it signals strong market sentiment continuing in the direction of the closing price [5].
3. **Body Spread Intensity:** A narrow body indicates weak market sentiment, whereas a wide body represents strong market sentiment [5].
4. **Location/Context Dependency:** A candle of the same physical shape has completely different meanings depending on where it appears in a trend or consolidation phase (e.g., a hammer shape at the bottom of a trend indicates strength, but at the top of a trend it is a "hanging man" indicating weakness) [5, 6].
5. **Volume Validates Price:** Start by analyzing the price candle, then look to the volume bar to find confirmation or anomalies [5].

---

### 2. The Trio of Premier Candles
Coulling identifies three primary single-bar candle patterns that serve as the chief triggers for VPA analysis across all timeframes [7, 8]:

* **The Shooting Star:** Price action demonstrating **weakness** [7].
* **The Hammer:** Price action demonstrating **strength** [9].
* **The Long Legged Doji:** Price action demonstrating **indecision** [10].

---

### 3. Shooting Star Weakness Profiles & Volume Analysis

#### Definition & Price Action Profile
A Shooting Star forms when price opens, rallies higher during the session, and then falls back to close at or near the opening price as sellers overwhelm buyers [7]. 
* **Key Rule of Immediate Reversal:** The appearance of a Shooting Star **DOES NOT** signal an immediate, instant trend reversal [7]. It signals **POTENTIAL WEAKNESS** at that point in the price action [7].
* **The "No Anomaly" Rule for Shooting Stars:** Unlike other candles, there is **never an anomaly** with a Shooting Star because the price structure itself is inherently weak [11, 12]. Volume simply validates the *degree* and *relative strength* of that weakness [11-13].

#### Shooting Star Volume Variants

| Volume Profile | VPA Interpretation & Market Behavior |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Low Volume** | Signals a minor pause or temporary short-term pullback in an uptrend [11, 13]. *(Special Exception: In an established downtrend following a selling climax, a low-volume shooting star represents a test of demand by market insiders [14]).* |
| **Average Volume** | Signals genuine, stronger weakness; the ensuing pullback will likely be more pronounced [13, 15]. |
| **High / Ultra-High Volume** | Professional money / insiders / market makers are actively dumping inventory and selling out into retail demand [15]. Marks a major top or selling climax component [15, 16]. |

---

### 4. Multi-Bar Wick Failures (Consecutive Upper Wicks)
Coulling emphasizes that while a single shooting star signals potential weakness, **consecutive upper wicks in the same price zone exponentially increase bearish sentiment** [17, 18]:

* **Single Candle:** Baseline warning signal of weakness [18].
* **Two Consecutive Candles:** Confirms increasing weakness if the second candle exhibits higher volume than the first, proving that selling pressure is growing [17, 18].
* **Three Consecutive Shooting Stars at Resistance:** When three consecutive candles fail at the exact same price ceiling accompanied by **rising volume**, it confirms that the market is struggling heavily at resistance [16]. This setup frequently forms part of an insider distribution phase or selling climax [16].

---

### 5. Validating Breakouts vs. Minor Pullbacks

When a Shooting Star or upper wick appears during an uptrend, traders face a core question: *Is this a minor pullback or the start of a major trend reversal?* [19, 20] Coulling outlines a 5-step analytical framework to validate breakouts and distinguish reversals from minor pullbacks:

1. **Evaluate Broader Context & Prior Accumulation:** Markets rarely reverse immediately after breaking out of a long accumulation zone, especially if a successful low-volume test occurred prior to the move [21].
2. **Wait for Subsequent Bar Confirmation:** Do not trade on the first candle alone [20]. Wait for follow-through candles (e.g., narrow-spread up candles or additional shooting stars on rising volume) [20].
3. **Assess Price Congestion & Key Levels:** Check if the candle forms at historical resistance/support boundaries [20, 22]. The longer a market moves sideways in congestion, the greater the likelihood of a true breakout or reversal [22].
4. **Apply Multiple Timeframe Triangulation:** Zoom into faster timeframes to inspect the fine details [21, 23]. For instance, a single 1-hour shooting star might expand into two 15-minute shooting stars on high volume, providing faster confirmation of genuine weakness [23].
5. **Volume-Validated Breakout Rules:**
   * **Clear Water Rule:** A valid breakout requires a clear, visible gap between the closing price of the candle and the congestion ceiling/floor [24].
   * **Effort on Breakout:** Valid breakouts require **well-above-average and rising volume** to break away from congestion [25].
   * **Low-Volume Retests:** Subsequent pullbacks to test the former resistance/support level **MUST occur on low or falling volume** to remain valid [26-28]. High volume on a pullback signals a false breakout / insider trap [25, 28].

---

### 6. Author Analogies & Visual Metaphors

Coulling uses distinct real-world analogies to explain price behavior and market mechanics:

* **The Oil Tanker:** The market is like a supertanker; it possesses immense momentum and cannot turn on a dime [20, 29]. It must slow down, apply the brakes (stopping volume), pause, and gradually turn [20, 29].
* **Quicksand / Bog:** Pulling a market out of a sideways congestion zone requires massive effort, like dragging someone out of quicksand [25]. This effort MUST be reflected in high volume bars [25].
* **Rubber Bands (Not Rods of Steel):** Support and resistance lines are flexible like rubber bands with give, not rigid metal walls [24, 30].
* **Ripples in a Pond:** Trend changes originate on fast timeframes (e.g., 5-minute charts) and ripple outward into slower, dominant timeframes (15-minute, 30-minute, 1-hour) [31].

---

### 7. Core Rules Summary
* **Rule 1:** A single shooting star warns of weakness; volume tells you how serious that weakness is [7, 13].
* **Rule 2:** Volume and price work together under Wyckoff’s Law of Effort vs. Result [32, 33]. High volume with little price progress indicates insider distribution or resistance [34-36].
* **Rule 3:** Rising price + falling volume = an unsustainable move built on lack of insider support [37, 38].
* **Rule 4:** Valid breakouts require high volume; valid pullbacks require low volume [25, 27].
