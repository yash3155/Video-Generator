### 1. Rising Price with Decreasing Volume (Draining Demand)

#### **Core Concepts & Definitions**
* **Draining Demand Anomaly:** A multi-bar anomaly in an uptrend where prices continue to rise with expanding spreads, but accompanying volume progressively falls [1, 2]. 
* **Overbought Market Condition:** When volume falls while prices rise, it reveals that buying pressure is draining away, the market is overbought, and the price move is not supported by professional market operators (insiders/market makers) [2, 3].

---

#### **Formulas & Structural Logic**
* **Wyckoff’s Third Law (Effort vs. Result):** \\(\text{Effort (Volume)} = \text{Result (Price Spread)}\\) [4, 5].
* **Trend Validation Rule:** \\(\text{Rising Price} + \text{Rising Volume} = \text{Valid Bullish Trend}\\) [6, 7].
* **Draining Demand Formula:** \\(\text{Rising Price Spreads} + \text{Falling Volume} = \text{Anomaly / Market Weakness}\\) [1, 2].

---

#### **Real-World Case Study: Bar-by-Bar Breakdown (Figure 4.16)**
Anna Coulling outlines a four-candle bullish sequence that degrades into a severe structural anomaly [1, 8]:

* **Candle 1 (Validated):** A narrow spread up candle accompanied by low volume. Effort matches result; no anomaly [8].
* **Candle 2 (First Anomaly):** A modestly wider spread up candle accompanied by **high volume** [8]. 
  * *Analysis:* High effort failed to produce a wide price spread [9]. Insiders are selling out into retail demand, creating resistance at higher levels [3, 9].
* **Candle 3 (Two Anomalies):** A wide spread up candle, but with **lower volume** than Candle 2 [5, 9].
  * *Anomaly A:* Price spread widened, but volume dropped [9].
  * *Anomaly B:* Rising market with falling volume confirms buying pressure is draining away [2, 9].
* **Candle 4 (Two Anomalies Again):** An extremely wide spread up candle accompanied by **even lower volume** [2, 5].
  * *Analysis:* Total breakdown of Effort vs. Result [2]. Insiders have withdrawn from the move and are marking prices up on thin volume to trap retail buyers in weak long positions before a reversal [2, 10].

---

#### **Author Analogies & Market Mechanics**
* **The Market Makers' Trap:** Insiders keep marking prices higher to maintain the visual illusion of a healthy bull market while secretly unloading their inventory to unsuspecting retail traders [10].

---

#### **Key Rules**
1. **Initial Warning:** High volume on a modest spread up candle during a trend is the first signal of insider distribution [3, 9].
2. **Trend Confirmation:** An anomaly on a single candle must be confirmed by subsequent candles showing falling volume on rising prices [11].

### 2. Downward Waterfall with Exhausting Volume

#### **Core Concepts & Definitions**
* **Price Waterfall:** A rapid market sell-off characterized by successive down candles [12].
* **Exhausting Volume Anomaly:** Occurs during a price waterfall when price spreads continue to widen downward while volume steadily decreases [13, 14]. This signals that selling pressure is exhausting and the market is becoming oversold [12, 15].

---

#### **Formulas & Structural Logic**
* **Gravity Fallacy Rule:** Unlike physical gravity, financial markets require increasing volume (effort) to fall, just as they do to rise [16, 17].
* **Downtrend Validation Rule:** \\(\text{Falling Price} + \text{Rising Volume} = \text{Valid Bearish Trend}\\) [17, 18].
* **Exhausting Waterfall Formula:** \\(\text{Falling Price Spreads} + \text{Falling Volume} = \text{Anomaly / Selling Exhaustion}\\) [14].

---

#### **Real-World Case Study: Bar-by-Bar Breakdown (Figure 4.17)**
Coulling analyzes a four-candle price waterfall to illustrate how volume exposes false breakdown moves [12, 13]:

* **Candle 1 (Validated):** A narrow spread down candle accompanied by low volume. Price and volume agree [12].
* **Candle 2 (First Anomaly):** A marginally wider spread down candle with **high to very high volume** [12].
  * *Analysis:* Massive effort (volume) produced only a minor price drop [12, 15]. Buyers (insiders) have stepped in to absorb supply, indicating market resistance to lower prices [12, 19].
* **Candle 3 (Two Anomalies):** A wide spread down candle accompanied by **lower/average volume** [14].
  * *Anomaly A:* Wide price spread on insufficient volume [14].
  * *Anomaly B:* Falling volume in a falling market confirms that bearish pressure is draining [14].
* **Candle 4 (Two Anomalies Again):** A wide spread down candle on **even lower volume** [14].
  * *Analysis:* Confirms that insiders bought their inventory on Candle 2 and are simply marking prices lower to trap retail traders into weak short positions [14, 19].

---

#### **Author Analogies**
* **Physical Gravity vs. Market Physics:** Traders often mistakenly believe that falling markets drop under their own weight; however, market operators must exert effort (volume) to drive prices down [16, 17].

---

#### **Key Rules**
1. **Absorption Signal:** High volume on a narrow or modest down candle during a sell-off signals insider buying (stopping/absorption volume) [12, 19].
2. **Waterfall Exhaustion:** Successive down candles on diminishing volume indicate that the bearish move is unsustainable [14].

---

### 3. Wyckoff's Micro-Macro-Global 3-Step Analytical Lens

#### **Core Concepts & Definitions**
* **3-Step Analytical Framework:** A structured, multi-tier process for evaluating market context by expanding perspective from an individual candle to the entire chart [20, 21].

---

#### **The 3-Step Process Breakdown**

| Step | Scope | Analytical Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1: Micro** | Single Candle | Analyzes each price bar as it arrives to determine whether volume validates or invalidates the spread (categorizing volume as low, average, high, or ultra-high relative to recent history) [20]. |
| **Step 2: Macro** | Adjacent Candles | Analyzes the current candle against the context of the last few candles to identify minor trends, minor anomalies, or short-term pullbacks [20, 22]. |
| **Step 3: Global** | Complete Chart | Zooms out to evaluate the entire chart structure, placing price action relative to key support/resistance levels, trend lines, and major chart patterns [20]. |

---

#### **Author Analogies**
* **Camera Zoom Lens in Reverse:** VPA begins zoomed in on a single subject (Micro) and gradually zooms out to capture the complete picture (Macro to Global) [21].
* **Ripples in a Pond:** Market sentiment originates at the micro level and ripples outward across timeframes [23]. Ripples in slower timeframes carry the greatest momentum and longest-term impact [23].
* **Russian Matryoshka Dolls:** Smaller market cycles (micro/macro phases on fast timeframes) fit inside larger market cycles (global phases on higher timeframes) [23, 24].

---

#### **Key Rules**
1. **Context Dependency:** A micro/macro anomaly on a few candles usually results only in a minor pullback unless backed by a larger global cause (Wyckoff's Second Law: Cause and Effect) [13, 22].
2. **Triangulation:** Never trade a single candle anomaly in isolation; validate it against adjacent bars (Macro) and key technical structural levels (Global) [6, 20, 25].
